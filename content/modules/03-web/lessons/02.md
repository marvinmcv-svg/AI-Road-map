## Lesson objectives
By the end, the student can…
- Name the 6 main parts of a landing page, in order.
- Explain the **visitor path**: from "What is this?" to "I want it!"
- Draw a **wireframe** for a phone and a laptop.

## Words for today
- **Landing page** — one page made for one goal, like "buy this" or "sign up".
- **Hero** — the big top part people see first.
- **Above the fold** — what you see before you scroll.
- **Social proof** — reasons to trust you, like real reviews or numbers.
- **Wireframe** — a simple box sketch of a page, no colors or pictures yet.
- **Footer** — the bottom part: contact, links, rules.

## 1. Warm-up — 5-second page test (10 min · 1-on-1: 5 min)
```teacher
Before builders build a house, they draw a plan. Today you plan your landing page. It is a page with one goal. Let's look at some real ones fast.
```

Show each of the 3 real landing pages for 5 seconds. Then hide it. Ask: "What does it sell? What do they want you to do?" Which page made it easy?

```solo
The student does the test, then explains why the "great" page was easy. Ask them to point to the very first thing their eyes landed on.
```

## 2. Mini-lesson — The landing page recipe (15 min · 1-on-1: 10 min)
Show **Visual 1**. A landing page tells a story from top to bottom:

| Part | Visitor thinks… | What goes there |
|---|---|---|
| 1. **Hero** (above the fold) | "What is this?" | Big headline, one line under it, a main picture or your video, one big button |
| 2. **Reasons** | "Why should I care?" | 3 benefits with icons |
| 3. **How it works** | "Is it easy?" | 3 simple steps (your explainer video!) |
| 4. **Proof** | "Can I trust you?" | Real reviews, real numbers, a promise (only true ones!) |
| 5. **Call to action** | "Okay, I want it!" | The button again, plus the price |
| 6. **Footer** | "Who are you?" | Contact, social links, privacy rules |

**One page, one goal.** Every part pushes toward the same button.

**Phone first.** Most people will see your page on a phone, so sketch the phone version first. Everything stacks in one long column.

## 3. Hands-on build — Wireframe your page (35 min · 1-on-1: 25 min)
1. Write your page's **one goal** (like "Order a bottle" or "Join the waitlist").
2. On the phone sheet, draw boxes for all 6 parts, top to bottom. Write a few words in each box (not full sentences yet).
3. Mark where your logo, launch video and product pictures go.
4. Draw the laptop version. What sits side by side now?
5. Ask Claude to check it: "Here's my landing page plan for [company]: [list your sections]. What is missing or confusing for [buyers]? Give 3 ideas."

**Checkpoint 1 (+20 XP):** phone wireframe with all 6 parts.
**Checkpoint 2 (+20 XP):** laptop wireframe + Claude feedback written down, with one change made.

```group
Pairs swap wireframes and play "visitor": point to where you'd click.
```

```solo
You are the visitor. Follow the wireframe top to bottom and say what you think at each part, like "What is this?… OK, why should I care?…" Wherever you get lost, they add or fix a box.
```

## 4. Game / challenge — Section Safari (15 min · 1-on-1: 10 min)
Look at 3 real landing pages. Find each of the 6 parts. Which page is missing a part? Which part is the weakest?

```group
Houses race to label every part on printouts. Most found: +15 XP. Everyone: +5.
```

```solo
**Student vs. Bolt.** Each of you picks a real landing page the other hasn't seen. Swap and find all 6 parts in 2 minutes. +15 XP for playing, +5 for finding them all.
```

## 5. Share-out — Plan pitch (10 min · 1-on-1: 5 min)
Walk through your wireframe in 60 seconds: "First you see… then… then you click…"

```solo
Time the pitch. Then ask: "If they only read the hero, would they know what you sell?"
```

## 6. Exit ticket (5 min)
1. Name the 6 parts of a landing page, in order.
2. What does "above the fold" mean?
3. Why sketch the phone version first?

```solo
Ask out loud. Let them point to each part on their own wireframe.
```

## Visuals to show
**Visual 1 — Landing page recipe.**
```prompt
A tall landing page diagram on a phone and a laptop, 16:9 canvas. Six stacked colored blocks labeled HERO, REASONS, HOW IT WORKS, PROOF, CALL TO ACTION, FOOTER, each with tiny gray placeholder lines and icons. A dashed line near the top labeled "the fold". Beside each block a small thought bubble: "What is this?", "Why care?", "Is it easy?", "Can I trust it?", "I want it!", "Who are you?". Flat clean design, bright colors, readable labels.
```

**Visual 2 — Wireframe example.**
```prompt
A hand-drawn style wireframe sketch on graph paper, 16:9, showing a phone screen and a laptop screen side by side for a made-up water bottle store. Simple boxes with an X for pictures, wavy lines for text, rounded rectangles for buttons labeled "Order now". No colors except a light blue pen look. Clean and readable.
```

**Visual 3 — One page, one goal.**
```prompt
A simple poster, 16:9, titled "ONE PAGE, ONE GOAL". A tall page with arrows from every section all pointing down to one big glowing button labeled "ORDER NOW". Flat design, bright friendly colors, bold readable text.
```

## Differentiation
- **Extra help:** give a wireframe sheet with the 6 boxes already drawn and labeled. Fill in the words only.
- **Extra challenge:** add a second goal for a different visitor (like "wholesale for shops") and plan how to keep it from stealing attention from the main button.
- **1-on-1 tip:** find a landing page for something the student actually bought online. Ask what made them trust it.
