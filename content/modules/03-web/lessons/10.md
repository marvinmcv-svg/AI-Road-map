## Lesson objectives
By the end, the student can…
- Run a short **user test** with a script, without helping the tester.
- Write down what happened: tasks done, time, where people got stuck.
- Sort feedback and pick the 3 fixes that matter most, then make them.

## Words for today
- **User test** — watching a real person try to use your site.
- **Task** — a job you ask the tester to do ("Buy a teal bottle").
- **Think aloud** — the tester says what they're thinking as they click.
- **Feedback** — what people tell you about your work.
- **Fix list** — the changes you'll make, most important first.

## 1. Warm-up — Guess the trouble (10 min · 1-on-1: 5 min)
Look at your own site. Guess: where will a new visitor get stuck? Write your guess down. You'll check it later.

```solo
You both write a guess for the student's site. Fold the papers. Open them after the tests.
```

## 2. Mini-lesson — The User Test Script (10 min · 1-on-1: 7 min)
Show **Visual 1**. Read this to each tester:
1. "Thanks for helping! We're testing the site, not you. You can't do anything wrong."
2. "Please think out loud as you go."
3. Give **3 tasks**, one at a time:
   - "Tell me what this company sells." (after 5 seconds on the home page)
   - "Find a product you like and order it." (using the test checkout)
   - "Find out how to return a product."
4. **Don't help.** Don't explain. Just watch, time it and take notes. If they're really stuck after 1 minute, move on.
5. End with the 4-question survey (Google Form):
   - How easy was it to order? (1–5)
   - What was confusing?
   - What did you like most?
   - Would you trust this store? Why or why not?

```teacher
The hardest part of a user test is staying quiet. When they get stuck, your mouth will want to help. Don't! Every time they get stuck, you just found something to fix.
```

## 3. Hands-on build — Test and fix (45 min · 1-on-1: 31 min)
1. Make the 4-question survey in Google Forms (5 min).
2. Run tests with 2 or 3 people. For each task note: done? how long? where stuck? what they said.
3. Paste your notes and survey answers into Gemini: "Sort this feedback into themes. What are the 3 biggest problems, and what fix would you try for each?" Then decide yourself.
4. Write your **Fix list** (most important first). Make the top 3 fixes. Republish.
5. Run the Works for Everyone and Trust checklists one more time.

**Checkpoint 1 (+20 XP):** 2 or more tests run with notes and survey answers.
**Checkpoint 2 (+20 XP):** top 3 fixes made, site republished.

```group
Test with students from another house. Testers move around the room.
```

```solo
Be the first tester yourself (act like a real new visitor). Then help set up a video call with one or two outside testers. The student runs the test; you stay quiet too!
```

## 4. Game / challenge — Biggest Fix Wins (10 min · 1-on-1: 7 min)
Look at your fix list. If you could only fix ONE thing, which would make the most people buy? Say why in one sentence.

```group
Each house shares one "biggest fix". The class votes for the smartest choice: +15 XP. Everyone: +5.
```

```solo
**Student vs. Bolt.** You both pick the biggest fix for the student's site, secretly. Reveal. If you agree, both win. If not, debate for 1 minute. +15 XP for playing, +5 for a good reason.
```

## 5. Share-out — Before and after (10 min · 1-on-1: 5 min)
Show one fix with a before and after. Open your warm-up guess. Were you right about where people got stuck?

```solo
Open the folded guesses. Who was closer? Talk about why it's so hard to see your own site with fresh eyes.
```

## 6. Exit ticket (5 min)
1. Why shouldn't you help a tester?
2. What was the biggest problem testers found?
3. What surprised you most?

```solo
Ask out loud. Next lesson is launch day. Write a short to-do list for it together.
```

## Differentiation
- **Extra help:** test with 1 person and use just 2 tasks.
- **Extra challenge:** try an A/B test: make 2 versions of the hero headline and test each with different people. Which got more "orders"?
- **1-on-1 tip:** a video call with screen sharing makes it easy to find outside testers. Record it (with the tester's OK) so the student can rewatch it.
