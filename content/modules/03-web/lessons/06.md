## Lesson objectives
By the end, the student can…
- Fix their site so it looks good and works on a phone.
- Write **alt text** for every important picture.
- Check color **contrast** and use clear headings and link words.
- Use a **screen reader** to test their page.

## Words for today
- **Accessible** — easy to use for everyone, including people with disabilities.
- **Screen reader** — an app that reads the screen out loud for people who can't see it well.
- **Alt text** — a short description of a picture, read out by screen readers.
- **Contrast** (again) — how much words stand out from the background.
- **Heading** — a title for a section. Screen readers use headings to jump around.
- **Mobile** — on a phone.

## 1. Warm-up — Eyes closed (10 min · 1-on-1: 5 min)
Turn on a screen reader. Close your eyes. Try to find the "Order" button on a website using only sound. Was it easy? What helped? What got in the way?

```group
One volunteer tries; the class watches and gives tips.
```

```solo
The student tries with eyes closed while you watch the screen. Then you try. Talk about how it felt.
```

## 2. Mini-lesson — Works for Everyone (15 min · 1-on-1: 10 min)
In the US, about 1 in 4 adults has some kind of disability (CDC). And everyone has a "bad moment": bright sun on a phone, a broken arm, slow internet. Accessible sites work better for **everyone**, and more people can buy from you.

**The checklist** (**Visual 1**):
1. 📱 **Phone view:** nothing squished, buttons big enough for a thumb, no sideways scrolling.
2. 🖼️ **Alt text:** describe what matters in the picture in one short sentence. ("Teal water bottle with ice on a mossy rock.") Skip "picture of". Pictures that are just for looks get no alt text.
3. 🌗 **Contrast:** check text color vs. background in the contrast checker. Aim for a pass on "AA".
4. 🔠 **Headings in order:** one big title, then section headings. Don't just make text big and bold.
5. 🔗 **Link and button words:** "See the colors", not "click here".
6. 🎬 **Captions on videos** (you did this in Module 2!).

**Gemini can help:** upload a picture and ask "Write alt text for this in under 15 words, for a website selling it." Then check that it's true.

## 3. Hands-on build — Fix your site (35 min · 1-on-1: 25 min)
Go through the checklist on your own site:
1. Phone preview: fix 2 or more things.
2. Alt text on every important picture (in Sites: click the picture → ⋮ → Alt text).
3. Check every text color pair; fix any that fail.
4. Use real headings for section titles.
5. Rename any "click here" link or button.
6. Republish.

**Checkpoint 1 (+20 XP):** phone fixes + alt text on every important picture.
**Checkpoint 2 (+20 XP):** contrast, headings and link words fixed; site republished.

```group
Pairs swap sites and tick the checklist for each other.
```

```solo
Do the first alt text together, then the student does the rest. Ask: "If you couldn't see this picture, what would you need to know?"
```

## 4. Game / challenge — Screen Reader Race (15 min · 1-on-1: 10 min)
With a screen reader on, find the price and the order button on a site. Time it.

```group
Each house tests another house's site. Fastest time (after fixes): +15 XP. Everyone: +5.
```

```solo
**Student vs. Bolt.** You race on the student's site; they race on yours. Then the student fixes whatever slowed you down and you race again. +15 XP for playing, +5 if their second time is faster.
```

## 5. Share-out — Before and after (10 min · 1-on-1: 5 min)
Show one fix you're proud of (a before and after).

```solo
The student shows you their best fix and explains who it helps.
```

## 6. Exit ticket (5 min)
1. What is alt text? Write one for your hero picture.
2. Why is "click here" a bad button?
3. Name 3 things on the Works for Everyone checklist.

```solo
Ask out loud. Keep the checklist; you'll use it again in lesson 10.
```

## Differentiation
- **Extra help:** do just phone view, alt text and button words today.
- **Extra challenge:** run a free accessibility checker (like the WAVE browser extension) on the site and fix the top 3 issues it finds.
- **1-on-1 tip:** if the student uses glasses, captions, or zoom themselves, start with their own experience.
