## Lesson objectives
By the end, the student can…
- Write a headline that says what it is and why it matters, in 10 words or less.
- Turn **features** into **benefits**.
- Write button words that say what happens next.
- Keep every claim true (no fake reviews, no "best in the world" without proof).

## Words for today
- **Copy** — the words on a website or ad.
- **Headline** — the big title at the top.
- **Feature** — a fact about the product ("steel bottle").
- **Benefit** — what it does for YOU ("your water stays cold all day").
- **Claim** — something you say is true about your product.

## 1. Warm-up — Clear or confusing? (10 min · 1-on-1: 5 min)
Read 5 real headlines. For each one: do you know what they sell? Thumbs up or down. Then fix one confusing headline together.

```solo
The student rewrites the most confusing headline. Then you rewrite it too. Compare.
```

## 2. Mini-lesson — The 4 copy rules (12 min · 1-on-1: 8 min)
Show **Visual 1**:
1. **Clear beats clever.** "Water bottles that never leak" beats "Hydration, reimagined."
2. **Benefits beat features.** Feature: "double steel walls." Benefit: "ice-cold water for 24 hours." Use both: the benefit first, the feature as proof.
3. **Buttons say what happens.** "Order my bottle" beats "Submit". "Join the waitlist" beats "Click here".
4. **Only true claims.** No fake reviews, no fake "only 2 left!", no "#1" unless you can prove it. Early on, use honest proof: "Made by students", "Free returns", "Tested in our backpacks for a month".

**Using Gemini well:** ask for 10 options, then you choose and change. Don't just paste the first answer. It should sound like your brand's personality words.

## 3. Hands-on build — Write your page copy (38 min · 1-on-1: 27 min)
In the 'Page Copy' doc, one section at a time:
1. **Hero:** ask Gemini for 10 headlines. Pick one, make it yours. Write one line under it, and the button words.
2. **Reasons:** list 3 features, then turn each into a benefit with a short title.
3. **How it works:** 3 steps, one short sentence each.
4. **Proof:** write 2 honest trust lines (no fake reviews).
5. **Call to action:** the price and the button again.
6. **Footer:** contact email (a brand email, not a personal one), a short "about us".
7. Ask Gemini: "Check this copy. Point out anything that sounds untrue or hard to read." Fix it.

**Checkpoint 1 (+20 XP):** hero + reasons written.
**Checkpoint 2 (+20 XP):** all 6 sections written and checked for honesty.

```group
Pairs read each other's hero out loud. If the listener can't say what it sells, rewrite.
```

```solo
Read each section out loud together. Anything that makes either of you stumble gets cut or rewritten. Ask "Is that true?" for every claim.
```

## 4. Game / challenge — Feature to Benefit (15 min · 1-on-1: 10 min)
Draw a feature card ("solar charger", "made of bamboo", "app with reminders"). Turn it into a benefit in 30 seconds.

```group
Houses take turns. Best benefit each round (class vote): +15 XP for the most wins. Everyone: +5.
```

```solo
**Student vs. Bolt.** Same card, both write a benefit. Swap and each picks the one that would make you buy. Then the student invents a feature card for you. +15 XP for playing, +5 for 3 or more wins.
```

## 5. Share-out — Radio read (10 min · 1-on-1: 5 min)
Read the hero and the call to action like a radio ad. Listeners say the one word they remember.

```solo
Read it to a guest on a call if you can. Which word did they remember? Is it the right one?
```

## 6. Exit ticket (5 min)
1. What's the difference between a feature and a benefit? Give an example.
2. Rewrite this button: "Submit".
3. Why is a fake review a problem, even a "small" one?

```solo
Ask out loud. Keep the Page Copy doc open. You'll use it next lesson.
```

## Differentiation
- **Extra help:** give fill-in frames: "[Product] that [benefit], so you can [feeling]." Write the hero and reasons only.
- **Extra challenge:** write 2 headlines for 2 kinds of buyers (students vs. hikers). Plan how you'd test which works better.
- **1-on-1 tip:** ask the student to explain their product to you as if you were a friend at lunch. Write down their exact words. That's often the best copy.
