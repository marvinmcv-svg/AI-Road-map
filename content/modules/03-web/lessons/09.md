## Lesson objectives
By the end, the student can…
- Spot **dark patterns** (design tricks) and remove any from their store.
- Write a short, true **privacy note** and store rules in plain words.
- Turn on **2-step verification** for the accounts that run their store.
- Spot 4 signs of a fake store or scam.

## Words for today
- **Dark pattern** — a sneaky design that tricks people into buying or signing up.
- **Privacy note (privacy policy)** — a page that says what info you collect and what you do with it.
- **Store rules (terms)** — the rules for buying: shipping, returns, refunds.
- **2-step verification** — a second check (like a code on your phone) when you log in.
- **Phishing** — fake messages that try to steal your password or money.

## 1. Warm-up — Spot the trick (10 min · 1-on-1: 5 min)
Show the 4 tricky shop designs. What's the trick in each? How does it make you feel?

```solo
The student names each trick. Then they think of one they've seen in a real app or store.
```

## 2. Mini-lesson — Building trust (15 min · 1-on-1: 10 min)
Show **Visual 1**, the **Trust Checklist**:
1. 🙅 **No dark patterns:** no fake timers, no hidden fees, no pre-ticked boxes, no guilt buttons.
2. 📄 **Privacy note:** what you collect, why, who sees it, how long you keep it, how to ask you to delete it.
3. 📦 **Store rules:** shipping time and cost, returns, refunds, contact.
4. 📧 **Real contact:** a brand email that someone reads.
5. 🔐 **Locked accounts:** strong passwords + 2-step verification on Google and Stripe.
6. 🏷️ **AI labels:** say which pictures are AI-made.

**Fake stores and scams** (**Visual 2**): prices too good to be true, no contact info, a weird web address, pressure to "pay now", payment by gift card. Phishing emails pretend to be your bank, Google or a delivery company.

```teacher
A store runs on trust. One trick might make one extra sale, but when buyers notice, they leave and they tell their friends. Honest stores win in the long run.
```

**About legal stuff:** this is a class project. A real business may need more (like taxes and local laws). If you ever sell for real, look up your local rules or ask an expert.

## 3. Hands-on build — Make it trustworthy (30 min · 1-on-1: 20 min)
1. Check your whole store against the Trust Checklist. Remove any trick.
2. Ask Claude: "Write a short privacy note in plain words for a small online store that collects: [list exactly what your form collects]. Say why, who sees it, how long we keep it, and how to ask us to delete it." Read every line. Delete anything that isn't true for you.
3. Write store rules (shipping, returns, refunds, contact) the same way.
4. Add a "Privacy & Rules" page and link it in the footer. Deploy again.
5. Turn on 2-step verification for your Google account (and Stripe, if you used it).

**Checkpoint 1 (+20 XP):** no dark patterns left, Privacy & Rules page live.
**Checkpoint 2 (+20 XP):** 2-step verification on, every line of the privacy note checked as true.

```solo
Read the privacy note together, one line at a time. For each line ask: "Do we really do this?" If not, cut it.
```

## 4. Game / challenge — Trust or Trick? (20 min · 1-on-1: 15 min)
Read each card. **Trust**, **Trick** or **Scam**? Say why.
1. "Only 2 left!" on a product you have 500 of. → Trick
2. A store with a real email, clear returns and AI labels on pictures. → Trust
3. An email from "Googel Support" asking you to log in from a link. → Scam (phishing)
4. A $4.99 "service fee" that shows up only on the last step. → Trick
5. Sneakers for $9 (usually $150), pay by gift card only. → Scam
6. A "Subscribe to our emails" box that's already ticked. → Trick
7. A review section with only real reviews, even a 3-star one. → Trust
8. A text saying your package is stuck, "pay $1.99 here". → Scam

```group
Houses get 2 minutes per card. Best reasons: +15 XP. Everyone: +5.
```

```solo
**Hot seat.** The student answers each card in 10 seconds with a reason. Then swap: they read and you answer, and you get one wrong on purpose. +15 XP for playing, +5 for catching you.
```

## 5. Share-out — The Honest Store promise (10 min · 1-on-1: 5 min)
Add a line to your Safety Pact: "When I sell online, I promise to…" Read it out loud.

```solo
The student reads their promise. Add it to their pact.
```

## 6. Exit ticket (5 min)
1. Name 2 dark patterns.
2. What 3 things should a privacy note say?
3. Name 3 signs of a fake store or scam.

```solo
Ask out loud. Check that the footer link to Privacy & Rules works.
```

## Differentiation
- **Extra help:** use a privacy note template with blanks to fill. Focus on removing tricks and adding the footer link.
- **Extra challenge:** find a real store with a dark pattern and write a short, polite note explaining how they could fix it (+25 XP side quest).
- **1-on-1 tip:** check the student's own email for a real phishing message (with their OK). Real examples are the best teacher.
