## Lesson objectives
By the end, the student can…
- Explain the **game loop**: input → update → draw, many times a second.
- Ask Claude to build a small playable game as an artifact.
- Change how it feels by tuning numbers (speed, size, spawn rate) and adding one new mechanic.

## Words for today
- **Game loop** — the steps a game repeats many times a second: check the buttons (input), move things (update), show them (draw).
- **Frame** (again) — one picture of the game. About 60 frames a second feels smooth.
- **Sprite** — a picture of one thing in the game (a player, a coin, an enemy).
- **Collision** — when two things in the game touch.
- **Spawn** — when something new appears in the game.
- **Tuning** — changing numbers to make a game feel better.

## 1. Warm-up — Play the demo (10 min · 1-on-1: 5 min)
Play the "catch the falling stars" demo on a computer and on a phone. What's the goal, rules, challenge and feedback?

```solo
Play it against each other for high score. Then ask: "What would make it more fun?" Write the ideas down.
```

## 2. Mini-lesson — How a game runs (12 min · 1-on-1: 8 min)
Show **Visual 1**: the **game loop**, about 60 times every second:
1. **Input:** which keys or touches are pressed?
2. **Update:** move the player, move the stars, check **collisions** (did the basket touch a star?), add points.
3. **Draw:** clear the screen, draw everything in its new place.
Then do it again. That's why games feel alive!

**Numbers make the feel** (**Visual 2**): player speed, how fast stars fall, how often new ones **spawn**, how big things are. Small changes make a big difference.

**Prompting games well:** describe the goal, controls (keyboard AND touch), what you collect or avoid, how you win or lose, and the look. Ask for **a few numbers at the top of the code** that are easy to change.

## 3. Hands-on build — Your first game (38 min · 1-on-1: 27 min)
1. Ask Claude for a tiny game as an artifact:
```
Make a small, original browser game as an artifact: [your idea, like "a cat catching falling fish"].
Goal: [goal]. Controls: arrow keys AND touch (tap left/right side of the screen).
Lose when [rule]. Show the score and a "Play again" button.
Keep it simple, bright and cute. Put the important numbers (speed, spawn rate, sizes) at the top so I can change them easily.
```
2. Play it 3 times. Write what feels too easy, too hard or boring.
3. **Tune it:** ask for one number change at a time ("Make the fish fall 20% slower at the start"). Play after each change.
4. **Add one mechanic:** a power-up, a bad item to avoid, or a combo bonus.
5. Publish it and play it on your phone.

**Checkpoint 1 (+20 XP):** playable game with a score and play again.
**Checkpoint 2 (+20 XP):** 2 tuning changes + 1 new mechanic, written in the Prompt Log.

```group
Pairs play each other's games and suggest one tuning change.
```

```solo
Be the player who says everything out loud: "Too slow… now too fast… ooh, that's fun!" The student uses your words to tune.
```

> 🛡️ **Original work moment:** make your OWN games. Use classic ideas (catching, jumping, dodging), but never copy a real game's name, characters or art.

## 4. Game / challenge — Score Attack (15 min · 1-on-1: 10 min)
Play each other's games for 2 minutes each. Highest score on each game wins a point. The maker gets a point if players say "one more try!"

```group
Most points: +15 XP. Everyone: +5.
```

```solo
**Student vs. Bolt.** Play each other's games: best of 3 rounds. The maker gets a bonus point for every "one more try!" +15 XP for playing, +5 for the win.
```

## 5. Share-out — One change, big difference (10 min · 1-on-1: 5 min)
Show one number you changed and how it changed the feel.

```solo
The student plays the game before and after their best change and explains what happened.
```

## 6. Exit ticket (5 min)
1. What are the 3 steps of the game loop?
2. What's a collision?
3. Which number change made the biggest difference in your game?

```solo
Ask out loud. Save the artifact link in the portfolio.
```

## Differentiation
- **Extra help:** start from the demo game and only tune numbers. Add a mechanic next time.
- **Extra challenge:** add levels that speed up every 10 points, and a high score that stays after refresh.
- **1-on-1 tip:** play a lot. Short build → play → tweak loops teach more than long builds.
