## Lesson objectives
By the end, the student can…
- Name the 4 parts every game has: **goal, rules, challenge, feedback**.
- Find the **core loop** of a game.
- Invent and test a simple paper game, then improve one rule.

## Words for today
- **Goal** — what the player is trying to do (reach the end, get the most points).
- **Rules** — what you can and can't do.
- **Challenge** — what makes it hard (time, enemies, puzzles).
- **Feedback** — how the game shows what's happening (points, sounds, a flash).
- **Core loop** — the small set of actions you repeat again and again (jump, collect, dodge).
- **Mechanic** — one thing the player can do (jump, shoot, match, build).

## 1. Warm-up — Play and pause (10 min · 1-on-1: 5 min)
Play 2 short games for 2 minutes each. After each one: "What were you trying to do? What kept you playing?"

```teacher
You've built a brand, a store, robot helpers and your own app. Now the final quest: you'll design and ship your own game. And you'll build it with Claude. First: what makes a game fun?
```

```group
Pairs play together and shout out the moment they wanted "one more try".
```

```solo
You both play the same game, then race to name its goal, its hardest part and the moment you wanted to play again.
```

## 2. Mini-lesson — The 4 parts and the core loop (15 min · 1-on-1: 10 min)
Show **Visual 1**:
| Part | Question | Example (a jumping game) |
|---|---|---|
| 🎯 **Goal** | What am I trying to do? | Reach the flag |
| 📜 **Rules** | What can I do, and not do? | Jump, run; don't touch spikes |
| 🧗 **Challenge** | What makes it hard? | Gaps, moving enemies, a timer |
| ✨ **Feedback** | How do I know how I'm doing? | A coin sound, a score, a flash when hit |

**The core loop** (**Visual 2**): the small cycle you repeat. *Run → jump → collect → avoid → repeat.* If the core loop is fun for 30 seconds, the game can be fun for an hour.

**Fun = a challenge that's just right.** Too easy = boring. Too hard = frustrating. Great games start easy and get harder as you get better.

## 3. Hands-on build — 15-minute paper game (35 min · 1-on-1: 25 min)
1. Pick 2 things from the box: dice, coins, cards, paper.
2. Invent a game: write the goal, 3 rules, the challenge and the feedback.
3. Play it 3 times. What's boring? What's too hard?
4. Change ONE rule and play again. Better or worse?
5. Ask Claude: "Here's my paper game: [rules]. What's the core loop? Suggest 3 small rule changes that could make it more fun." Try one.

**Checkpoint 1 (+20 XP):** paper game with goal, 3 rules, challenge and feedback, played 3 times.
**Checkpoint 2 (+20 XP):** one rule changed and tested, with a note on what changed.

```group
Pairs make one game together and play against each other.
```

```solo
Make a game together and play it against each other. Then each of you changes one rule in secret and you play again. Who made it more fun?
```

> 🛡️ **Fair play moment:** good games are fair: every player knows the rules, and luck doesn't decide everything.

## 4. Game / challenge — Game Swap (15 min · 1-on-1: 10 min)
Swap paper games. Read only the written rules (no explaining!). Play it, then rate: fun 1 to 5, rules clear 1 to 5.

```group
Highest total score: +15 XP. Everyone who plays 2 games: +5.
```

```solo
**Student vs. Bolt.** You play their game from the rules alone; they play yours. Where the rules were unclear, fix them. +15 XP for playing, +5 if their rules were clear on the first try.
```

## 5. Share-out — Pitch the loop (10 min · 1-on-1: 5 min)
Explain your game's core loop in one sentence: "You ___, then ___, then ___ again."

```solo
The student explains the core loop. Ask: "Which part would be the most fun to see on a screen?"
```

## 6. Exit ticket (5 min)
1. Name the 4 parts every game has.
2. What's the core loop of a game you love?
3. Why is "just right" challenge important?

```solo
Ask out loud. Keep the paper game. It might become a digital game later!
```

## Differentiation
- **Extra help:** start from a ready-made dice game and change just one rule.
- **Extra challenge:** design the paper game for 2 players who have different powers. Is it still fair?
- **1-on-1 tip:** ask about the student's all-time favorite game and take apart its 4 parts together. It makes the model click.
