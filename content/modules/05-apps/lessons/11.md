## Lesson objectives
By the end, the student can…
- Run a short **beta test** with a plan, tasks and a survey.
- Sort feedback into themes and pick the top fixes with an **impact vs. effort** grid.
- Ship **version 2** with clear **release notes**.

## Words for today
- **Beta test** — letting a few real people use your app before everyone else.
- **Beta tester** — a person who tries your app and gives feedback.
- **Impact** — how much a fix helps users.
- **Effort** — how much work a fix takes.
- **Release notes** — a short list of what's new and fixed in a version.

## 1. Warm-up — Guess the feedback (10 min · 1-on-1: 5 min)
```teacher
Today real people try your app. They will find things you missed. That is a gift, not a bad thing. Write down your guesses first.
```

Write down: what will testers love? What will they complain about? Fold the paper. You'll open it at the end.

```solo
You both write your guesses for the student's app. Open them at the share-out.
```

## 2. Mini-lesson — The beta test plan (10 min · 1-on-1: 7 min)
Show **Visual 1**:
1. **Invite** 3 or more testers. Send the link + how to add it to their home screen.
2. **Tasks:** "Please use it for 3 days to [main job]. Try [AI feature] at least twice."
3. **Survey** (5 questions):
   - How easy was it to use? (1–5)
   - What did you use it for most?
   - What was confusing or broken?
   - What did you like most?
   - Would you keep using it? Why or why not?
4. **Numbers:** how many entries did testers add? (Check your Supabase table, counts only, don't read private entries.)
5. **Sort and pick:** themes → **impact vs. effort** grid → fix the high-impact, low-effort ones first.

## 3. Hands-on build — Run the beta and ship v2 (45 min · 1-on-1: 31 min)
*Tip: send the app to testers a few days before this lesson, so feedback is ready.*
1. Collect the survey answers and your notes.
2. Paste the feedback (no names) into Claude: "Sort this feedback into themes. What are the 3 biggest problems, and how could each be fixed?" Then decide yourself.
3. Place each fix on the impact vs. effort grid. Pick the top 3.
4. Ask Claude Code to make the 3 fixes. Run your test checklist. Deploy version 2.
5. Write release notes (with Claude's help): "Version 2: New… Fixed… Thanks to our testers!"

**Checkpoint 1 (+20 XP):** 3 or more testers' feedback collected and sorted into themes.
**Checkpoint 2 (+20 XP):** top 3 fixes shipped as version 2, with release notes sent to testers.

```group
Pairs swap feedback themes and suggest one fix for each other.
```

```solo
Be one of the testers yourself, a few days before. In the lesson, help the student read the feedback without getting defensive: "Every complaint is a free idea."
```

> 🛡️ **Tester privacy moment:** testers' data is real. Only look at counts and their survey answers, never their private entries. Delete test accounts they don't want to keep.

## 4. Game / challenge — Impact vs. Effort (10 min · 1-on-1: 7 min)
Place 8 fix ideas on the grid in 3 minutes. Which 2 would you do first? Defend your choice.

```group
Best-defended choices (class vote): +15 XP. Everyone: +5.
```

```solo
**Student vs. Bolt.** You both place the same 8 fixes on the grid, secretly. Compare and debate the differences. +15 XP for playing, +5 for a strong argument.
```

## 5. Share-out — Release notes (10 min · 1-on-1: 5 min)
Read your release notes. Then open your warm-up guesses. Were you right?

```solo
Open the folded guesses. Which surprise taught the student the most?
```

## 6. Exit ticket (5 min)
1. What's a beta test for?
2. What is the impact vs. effort grid?
3. What did testers love that you didn't expect?

```solo
Ask out loud. Next lesson is the Boss: the App Launch Showcase.
```

## Differentiation
- **Extra help:** 2 testers and 3 survey questions. Fix the top 1 or 2 things.
- **Extra challenge:** add a tiny in-app "Send feedback" button that saves feedback to a Supabase table.
- **1-on-1 tip:** if the student is nervous about feedback, read the first answers together and celebrate the first compliment.
