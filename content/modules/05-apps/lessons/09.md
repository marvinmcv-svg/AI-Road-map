## Lesson objectives
By the end, the student can…
- Design the 5 **app states**: loading, empty, error, success and first time.
- Write friendly, helpful messages for each state.
- Add one small **moment of delight** without slowing the app down.

## Words for today
- **Loading state** — what you see while the app is waiting.
- **Empty state** — what you see when there's no data yet.
- **Error state** — what you see when something goes wrong, and how to fix it.
- **Onboarding** — the first-time welcome that shows a new user what to do.
- **Delight** — a small fun touch that makes people smile (a confetti burst for a 7-day streak).

## 1. Warm-up — The cold app (10 min · 1-on-1: 5 min)
```teacher
Little things make an app feel great or broken: loading, empty screens, errors. Today you fix them, and add a happy moment. Try this slow app.
```

Use the "bad feel" app on a slow network. How does it feel? Where did you think it was broken?

```solo
The student uses it and says out loud every moment they felt lost. Write each one down. That's the fix list.
```

## 2. Mini-lesson — The 5 states (12 min · 1-on-1: 8 min)
Show **Visual 1**:
| State | Bad | Good |
|---|---|---|
| ⏳ **Loading** | Blank screen | "Loading your week…" or a gentle spinner |
| 🫙 **Empty** | Nothing | "No entries yet. Tap + to log your first glass!" |
| ⚠️ **Error** | "Error 500" | "We couldn't save that. Check your internet and try again." + a Retry button |
| ✅ **Success** | Nothing happens | "Saved!" with a check, or a small moving effect |
| 👋 **First time** | Dropped into a full app | 2 or 3 short welcome screens, or tips on the first use |

**Good messages:** say what happened, why (simply), and what to do next. Never blame the user.

**Delight, done right:** small, fast, and tied to a real win (finishing a goal, a streak). Not on every tap.

## 3. Hands-on build — Polish pass (43 min · 1-on-1: 30 min)
1. Go through every screen and write down the 5 states for each one.
2. Ask Claude for message ideas in your app's voice, then pick and change them.
3. Ask Claude Code:
```
Polish my app's states. For each screen, add: a loading message, a friendly empty state with a next step, helpful error messages with a Retry button, success feedback after saving, and a 3-screen first-time welcome. Use these messages: [paste]. Add one small celebration when [goal] happens. Keep it fast and accessible.
```
4. Test with a slow network and with a brand-new account.
5. Run your test checklist again. Deploy.

**Checkpoint 1 (+20 XP):** all 5 states written down for every screen.
**Checkpoint 2 (+20 XP):** states built, tested on a slow network and with a new account.

```group
Pairs swap phones and rate each state 1 to 3.
```

```solo
Make a brand-new account on their app while they watch. Narrate your first-time experience out loud.
```

> 🛡️ **Honesty moment:** messages must be true. Don't say "Saved!" before the data is really saved, and don't hide errors.

## 4. Game / challenge — First-Timer Test (10 min · 1-on-1: 7 min)
A tester who has never seen your app gets one task. No help! Time how long until they finish, and count the moments they look lost.

```group
Fastest finish with no lost moments: +15 XP. Everyone: +5.
```

```solo
**Student vs. Bolt.** Find a guest (or a family member on a call) to be the first-timer for the student's app. Then the student is the first-timer for a demo app of yours. +15 XP for playing, +5 if their tester finishes with no help.
```

## 5. Share-out — Before and after (10 min · 1-on-1: 5 min)
Show one state before and after. Which change made the biggest difference?

```solo
Screen-record the before and after for their portfolio.
```

## 6. Exit ticket (5 min)
1. Name the 5 app states.
2. Rewrite this error: "Error 500".
3. When is delight a good idea, and when is it annoying?

```solo
Ask out loud. Add any new ideas to the Later list.
```

## Differentiation
- **Extra help:** do loading, empty and success first. Error and first time next lesson.
- **Extra challenge:** make the app work offline: save changes on the phone and sync when the internet is back. Show the user what's waiting to sync.
- **1-on-1 tip:** the student's own "bad app" stories from lesson 3 are perfect examples here.
