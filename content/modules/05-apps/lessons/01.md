## Lesson objectives
By the end, the student can…
- Say how an **app** is different from a website.
- Name 3 ways to make an app and why we'll build an **installable web app**.
- Write a clear **problem statement** for their own app, and make a first clickable prototype with Claude.

## Words for today
- **App** — a tool on your phone or computer that helps you DO something (track, plan, learn, play).
- **Native app** — an app from the App Store or Google Play, made just for iPhone or Android.
- **Installable web app** — a web app you can add to your home screen, so it opens like a real app. No app store needed.
- **User** — the person who uses your app.
- **Problem statement** — one sentence: who has what problem, and when.
- **Prototype** — a quick, rough version to try an idea.

## 1. Warm-up — My phone, my problems (10 min · 1-on-1: 5 min)
Open your phone. Pick 3 apps you use a lot. For each: what problem does it solve? Then: what problem do YOU have that no app solves well?

```teacher
Your store has a website and robot helpers. Now we build something just for YOU: your own app. By the end of this module, it will be on your phone's home screen, with your data, a login, and its own AI helper.
```

```group
Pairs share their apps and problems. Collect the best "no app solves this" problems on the board.
```

```solo
You both show your 3 apps. Then brainstorm 5 annoying problems from the student's week (school, work, money, health, hobbies).
```

## 2. Mini-lesson — 3 ways to make an app (15 min · 1-on-1: 10 min)
Show **Visual 1**:

| | Native app | Web app | Installable web app ✅ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Where it lives | App Store / Google Play | A web address | A web address + your home screen |
| Works on | iPhone OR Android | Any browser | Any phone or computer |
| How to share | Store review (days) | Send a link | Send a link, then "Add to Home Screen" |
| Good for | Games, heavy apps | Quick tools | **Our personal apps** |

**Why installable web apps?** One build works on every phone. No store approval. Claude Code can build it, and Vercel puts it online, just like your website.

**Good app ideas are small and specific:** not "a fitness app", but "a 10-second log of how much water I drank, with a streak."

**The 4 Boss options:** a **tracker** (habits, money, workouts), a **diary** (mood, gratitude, dreams), an **AI tutor** (quiz me on my class notes), a **scheduler** (study plan, shifts). Or your own idea!

## 3. Hands-on build — Problem → App (35 min · 1-on-1: 25 min)
1. Fill in the **Problem → App** sheet:
   - **Who:** "I, a college student who…"
   - **Problem:** "…forgets to drink water / spends too much on food / can't keep up with readings"
   - **When it happens:** "…during long study days"
   - **What the app does:** 3 main things, no more
   - **How you'll know it works:** "I drink 8 glasses on 5 days a week"
2. Ask Claude to build a quick prototype as an artifact:
```
Make a simple mobile app prototype as an artifact for this idea: [paste your sheet].
Just 2 or 3 screens, big buttons, a bottom tab bar. Use fake sample data. Keep it simple.
```
3. Open it, click around, and ask for 2 changes.
4. Publish the artifact and open it on your phone.

**Checkpoint 1 (+20 XP):** Problem → App sheet done, with only 3 main things.
**Checkpoint 2 (+20 XP):** clickable prototype open on your phone.

```group
Walk around and ask: "Would YOU use this every day? Why?"
```

```solo
Play the user: use the prototype on your own phone while the student watches. Say out loud what you'd expect to happen at each tap.
```

> 🛡️ **Privacy moment:** personal apps hold personal info (health, money, feelings). From day 1, think: "What's the least info this app needs?"

## 4. Game / challenge — App Idea Speed Round (15 min · 1-on-1: 10 min)
Draw a random "user" card (a nurse, a dog owner, a student athlete, a grandparent). You have 2 minutes to pitch an app that solves one of their problems in one sentence.

```group
Houses pitch; the class votes for the most useful idea. +15 XP to the winner. Everyone: +5.
```

```solo
**Student vs. Bolt.** Take turns drawing cards and pitching. The other person rates each idea 1 to 5 for "would they really use it?". +15 XP for playing, +5 for the higher total.
```

## 5. Share-out — 30-second pitch (10 min · 1-on-1: 5 min)
Pitch your own app: "For [who], who [problem], my app [does 3 things]."

```solo
The student pitches to you, then to a guest if you can. Ask: "What's the ONE thing it must do perfectly?"
```

## 6. Exit ticket (5 min)
1. What's the difference between a native app and an installable web app?
2. Write your problem statement in one sentence.
3. What's the least personal info your app needs?

```solo
Ask out loud. Keep the sheet; it's the plan for the whole module.
```

## Differentiation
- **Extra help:** pick one of the 4 Boss options and use a ready-made problem statement to edit.
- **Extra challenge:** interview 2 people who have the same problem. What would they need the app to do?
- **1-on-1 tip:** the best app ideas come from the student's real week. Spend the time to find a problem they actually feel.
