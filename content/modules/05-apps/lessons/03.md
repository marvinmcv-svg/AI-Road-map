## Lesson objectives
By the end, the student can…
- Use 6 mobile design rules: thumb zone, tab bar, big buttons, simple lists, short forms, feedback.
- Make a mini **style guide** and an **app icon**.
- Turn their paper screens into a clickable design with Claude.

## Words for today
- **Thumb zone** — the part of the screen your thumb reaches easily (the bottom half).
- **Tap target** — the area you can tap. Big is better (about the size of a fingertip).
- **Feedback** (in apps) — the app shows something happened: a check, a color change, a short message.
- **Style guide** — your app's rules: colors, font, button style, icon.
- **App icon** — the little picture on your home screen.

## 1. Warm-up — Easy or annoying? (10 min · 1-on-1: 5 min)
```teacher
Great apps feel easy, and that is no accident. Today you learn why. Think about where your thumb reaches. Which of these are annoying?
```

Look at 4 app screenshots. Which are easy to use? Which are annoying? Point to why.

```solo
The student shows you the most annoying app on their phone and explains why. Write down their reasons. Those become their own "don't" list.
```

## 2. Mini-lesson — 6 rules for thumbs (15 min · 1-on-1: 10 min)
Show **Visual 1**:
1. 👍 **Thumb zone:** put the main button at the bottom, where thumbs reach.
2. 🧭 **Tab bar:** 2 to 4 tabs at the bottom, with icons AND words.
3. 🔘 **Big tap targets:** buttons at least the size of a fingertip, with space between them.
4. 📋 **Simple lists:** one item per row, the most important info first.
5. ✍️ **Short forms:** ask only what you need; use pickers and buttons instead of typing.
6. ✅ **Feedback:** every tap shows something: a check, a sound, a short "Saved!" message.

**Plus 2 from earlier modules:** contrast (easy to read) and one clear "biggest thing" per screen.

**Style guide:** 2 colors (one main, one accent), 1 font, one button style, your icon.

## 3. Hands-on build — From paper to polished (35 min · 1-on-1: 25 min)
1. Write your mini style guide.
2. Make an app icon in Gemini: "Simple app icon of [symbol], [colors], flat, rounded square, no words, 1:1." Pick the best one.
3. Give Claude your paper screen photos and style guide:
```
Here are photos of my paper app screens and my style guide: [colors, font, button style].
Make a polished, clickable mobile app design as an artifact. Follow these rules: main buttons in the thumb zone, a bottom tab bar with icons and words, big tap targets, show feedback after every tap. Use fake sample data.
```
4. Check every screen against the 6 rules. Ask for fixes, one at a time.

**Checkpoint 1 (+20 XP):** style guide + app icon.
**Checkpoint 2 (+20 XP):** clickable design of every screen, checked against the 6 rules.

```group
Pairs check each other's designs with the 6-rule list.
```

```solo
Use the design on your phone with one hand while the student watches. Every time your thumb stretches, that's a fix.
```

## 4. Game / challenge — One-Thumb Test (15 min · 1-on-1: 10 min)
Hold a phone in one hand. Do the main task with just your thumb. Count the stretches and missed taps.

```group
Fewest stretches after one fix: +15 XP. Everyone: +5.
```

```solo
**Student vs. Bolt.** Each of you does the one-thumb test on the other's design. Fewest stretches wins. +15 XP for playing, +5 for the win.
```

## 5. Share-out — Design check (10 min · 1-on-1: 5 min)
Show your design and icon. Which rule are you proudest of following?

```solo
Put the icon next to your real home screen apps (a screenshot is fine). Does it stand out?
```

## 6. Exit ticket (5 min)
1. What's the thumb zone?
2. Name 4 of the 6 rules.
3. Why should every tap show feedback?

```solo
Ask out loud. Save the design link. Claude Code will use it next lesson.
```

## Differentiation
- **Extra help:** design only the Home and Add screens today. Use the 6 rules as a checklist.
- **Extra challenge:** add a dark mode to the design, and check contrast in both modes.
- **1-on-1 tip:** look at the student's favorite app together and copy one good idea (not its look) into their design.
