## Lesson objectives
By the end, the student can…
- Add **captions** and **title cards** that are big, short and easy to read.
- Explain why captions help everyone, including people who can't hear well.
- Make a short **explainer** clip that shows how the product works in 3 steps.

## Words for today
- **Captions** — the words of what's being said, shown at the bottom of the video.
- **Title card** — a screen with big words, like a title or a step number.
- **Explainer video** — a short video that shows how something works, step by step.
- **Safe zone** — the middle part of a phone video, where buttons won't cover your words.
- **Contrast** (again) — light words on dark, or dark on light, so words pop.

## 1. Warm-up — Mute test (10 min · 1-on-1: 5 min)
Play an ad on mute. Can you still tell what it's about? Then play one with captions on mute. Ask: "Why might people watch with the sound off?" (On the bus, in class, late at night, or they can't hear well.)

```solo
Play the student's own rough cut on mute. Can they still follow it? This sets up today's fix.
```

## 2. Mini-lesson — Words that work (12 min · 1-on-1: 8 min)
Show the bad example, then the fix (**Visual 1**):
- **Big:** easy to read on a phone.
- **Short:** 3 to 7 words on screen at once.
- **Long enough:** leave words up long enough to read twice.
- **Contrast:** white words with a dark edge, or a solid color bar behind them.
- **Safe zone:** for tall phone videos, keep words away from the top, bottom and right side, where buttons sit.
- **Spelled right:** check every caption. Auto captions can mishear words, and AI pictures can have wiggly letters.

**Explainer videos** (**Visual 2**) show how something works in 3 steps:
1. Title card: "How [product] works"
2. Step 1 → Step 2 → Step 3 (one short clip + one title card each)
3. End card: logo + call to action

```teacher
Captions aren't just nice to have. For people who are deaf or hard of hearing, they're the only way to get the message. Good captions make your video work for everyone.
```

## 3. Hands-on build — Captions + explainer (38 min · 1-on-1: 27 min)
**Part A — Fix and caption the ad (18 min · 1-on-1: 12 min):**
1. Fix the top 3 things on your fix list from last lesson.
2. Turn on captions in Google Vids. Check every word.
3. Add a title card for the hook (if it helps) and an end card with the logo + call to action.

**Checkpoint 1 (+20 XP):** the ad works on mute (captions + end card).

**Part B — 3-step explainer (20 min · 1-on-1: 15 min):**
1. Ask Gemini: "Explain how [my product] works in 3 simple steps, one short sentence each, for [buyers]." Check it's true and makes sense.
2. Make one clip per step in Gemini (same style as your ad).
3. Put it together in Vids with a title card for each step. 15 to 30 seconds is plenty.

**Checkpoint 2 (+20 XP):** explainer done, with title cards and captions.

```group
Pairs do the mute test on each other's ad before starting Part B.
```

```solo
For Part A, you read the captions out loud while the video plays. Mistakes jump out fast. For Part B, if clip limits are low, use still Gemini pictures with a slow zoom instead of video clips.
```

## 4. Game / challenge — 3-Second Mute Test (15 min · 1-on-1: 10 min)
Show someone the ad on mute. Stop it at the end. Ask: *What's the brand? What does it do? What should you do next?* All 3 right = win. If not, fix one thing and test again.

```group
Pairs rotate. All 3 right: +15 XP to the maker. Fixed and retested: +5.
```

```solo
Test on you, then on a guest. Then the student tests YOUR mute-test video (make one with a hidden mistake: a misspelled caption or words in the button zone). Can they spot it? +15 XP for playing, +5 for spotting your mistake.
```

## 5. Share-out — Caption check (10 min · 1-on-1: 5 min)
Play the explainer. Check with the list: big, short, long enough, contrast, safe zone, spelled right.

```solo
The student checks their own video with the list first, then you do. Where do you agree?
```

## 6. Exit ticket (5 min)
1. Give 2 reasons people watch videos on mute.
2. Name 3 rules for good on-screen words.
3. What are the parts of an explainer video?

```solo
Ask out loud. Then watch the finished ad one more time, on mute.
```

## Differentiation
- **Extra help:** captions only today. The explainer can be 3 still pictures with title cards.
- **Extra challenge:** make captions in a second language with Gemini's help, and check them with someone who speaks it.
- **1-on-1 tip:** pick a product step the student can act out with their hands while you film (hands only, no faces). Real shots mixed with AI clips look great.
