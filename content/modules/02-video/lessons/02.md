## Lesson objectives
By the end, the student can…
- Explain why the **hook** (the first 3 seconds) matters most.
- Plan a short ad with 4 parts: **Hook → Problem → Fix → Call to action**.
- Make a **storyboard** with a Gemini picture and a note for each shot.

## Words for today
- **Hook** — the first 3 seconds. It makes people stop and watch.
- **Story beats** — the main parts of a story, in order.
- **Storyboard** — a comic-strip plan of a video. One box for each shot.
- **Shot** — one piece of video between two cuts.
- **Call to action** (again) — what you want people to do at the end.

## 1. Warm-up — Stop or scroll? (10 min · 1-on-1: 5 min)
Play the first 3 seconds of 4 ads, then pause. For each one: "Would you keep watching, or scroll away?" Then ask: "What made you want to stay?"

```group
Everyone holds up ✋ (stop) or 👉 (scroll). Count the votes.
```

```solo
The student says "stop" or "scroll" for each one and gives a reason. Then they show YOU an ad they love (school-safe) and explain its hook.
```

## 2. Mini-lesson — The 4-part ad story (15 min · 1-on-1: 10 min)
Show **Visual 1**. Most short ads follow 4 beats:

| Beat | Time (for a 30-second ad) | What happens | Example (water bottle brand) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🪝 **Hook** | 0–3 s | Something surprising, funny or curious | A plant is "sweating" in the sun and gasps for water |
| 😩 **Problem** | 3–10 s | The buyer's problem | Your old bottle leaks and keeps water warm |
| ✨ **Fix** | 10–25 s | Your product saves the day | The bottle stays cold all day and never leaks |
| 👉 **Call to action** | 25–30 s | What to do now | "Grab yours at sipsquad.example" + logo |

**5 kinds of hooks:** a question ("Ever had a leaky bottle?"), a surprise (something odd happens), a sound (a big splash), a "POV:" moment, or a before-and-after.

Then show **Visual 2**: a storyboard. Each box has a picture, the action, the camera, the sound and the words on screen.

```teacher
Real movie makers don't start by filming. They start by drawing. A storyboard is your movie as a comic strip. It lets you fix the story before you use up any video clips.
```

## 3. Hands-on build — Storyboard your launch ad (35 min · 1-on-1: 25 min)
1. **Ideas (8 min):** ask Gemini: "Give me 5 hook ideas for a 30-second ad for [your company], for [your buyers]. Make them short and surprising." Pick one, or mix two.
2. **Beats (7 min):** write one sentence for each of the 4 beats.
3. **Storyboard (20 min):** make 4 to 6 boxes in Google Slides. For each box:
   - Make a picture in Gemini for that shot (use the Image Prompt Formula, same style every time).
   - Write under it: **Action · Camera · Sound · Words on screen**.

**Checkpoint 1 (+20 XP):** 4 beats written.
**Checkpoint 2 (+20 XP):** storyboard with 4 or more boxes, each with a picture and notes.

```group
Work alone or in pairs on one brand.
```

```solo
Play "pitch meeting": the student tells you the story out loud while pointing at each box. Ask "What does the viewer feel here?" for each one. If a box feels slow, cut it.
```

> 🛡️ **Honesty moment:** the "Fix" part must be true. No fake results and no "best in the world" unless you can prove it.

## 4. Game / challenge — Hook Factory (15 min · 1-on-1: 10 min)
Pick a boring product (socks, a pencil, a stapler). Write as many hooks as you can in 3 minutes. Use all 5 hook kinds. Then pick the best one and say why.

```group
Houses write hooks. The class votes for the best one. Best hook: +15 XP. Every house with 5 or more hooks: +5.
```

```solo
**Student vs. Bolt.** You both write hooks for the same product for 3 minutes. Read them out one at a time, back and forth. Each picks the other's best one. Then do a second round with a new product. +15 XP for playing, +5 for 5 or more hooks.
```

## 5. Share-out — Storyboard pitch (10 min · 1-on-1: 5 min)
Tell the story in 30 seconds, box by box. Listeners say which box hooked them most.

```solo
Time the pitch. Is it close to 30 seconds? If you have a guest (a family member or a video call), let the student pitch to them too.
```

## 6. Exit ticket (5 min)
1. What are the 4 beats of a short ad?
2. Write 2 different hooks for your brand.
3. Why make a storyboard before making clips?

```solo
Ask out loud. For #2, let them say the hooks like a voice actor.
```

## Differentiation
- **Extra help:** give a filled-in example storyboard to copy the layout from. 4 boxes is enough.
- **Extra challenge:** make a 15-second version and a 60-second version of the same story. What do you cut? What do you add?
- **1-on-1 tip:** the student's own favorite ads are the best teaching examples. Ask them to bring one.
