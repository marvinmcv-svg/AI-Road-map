## Lesson objectives
By the end, the student can…
- Say why AI **hallucinates**. (It guesses what sounds right, not what is true.)
- Use the **Two-Source Rule** to check a fact.
- Spot **bias** in AI pictures and say where it comes from.
- Explain age rules, **consent** and why **deepfakes** hurt people. Then make a Safety Pact.

## Words for today
- **Hallucination** — when AI says something false but sounds very sure.
- **Bias** — an unfair pattern. The AI repeats it because its examples were one-sided.
- **Terms of Service (ToS)** — the rules of an app. Many say you must be 13 or 18.
- **Consent** — asking first and getting a clear "yes".
- **Deepfake** — a fake picture, video or voice of a real person, made with AI.
- **Privacy** — keeping your own info safe.
- **Disclosure** — telling people you used AI.

## 1. Warm-up — Two Truths and a Hallucination (10 min · 1-on-1: 5 min)
Show 3 facts that Gemini wrote about an animal (like octopuses). One is made up. Which one? And **how** could you check? Then show the answer from a trusted source.

```teacher
Gemini wrote all three facts. It sounded just as sure about each one. It didn't lie on purpose. It guessed words that SOUND like an octopus fact. That's called a hallucination. Our job as creators is to check.
```

```group
Houses vote with whiteboards. Each house must name one way to check.
```

```solo
The student picks the fake one and gives a reason. Then they find the answer themselves on the trusted site while you watch. If time allows, do a second set where THEY make one up to fool you.
```

## 2. Mini-lesson — Three ways AI gets it wrong (15 min · 1-on-1: 10 min)
Show **Visual 1 (Mistake Detective poster)**:
1. **Hallucination** — sure, but false. *Fix:* the Two-Source Rule. Check 2 trusted places that agree.
2. **Bias** — show your 4 Gemini pictures ("a scientist", "a nurse"…). Ask: "What do you notice about who shows up?" If the examples mostly show one kind of person, the AI copies that pattern. *Fix:* notice it, ask for more kinds of people, and speak up.
3. **Out of date** — an AI only learned up to a certain day. It may not know new news. *Fix:* check the date on your source.

Then the **rules of the road** (**Visual 2**):
- **Age rules (ToS):** lots of AI apps are for ages 13+ or 18+. These laws protect kids' info. We use school accounts, or I run the tool for you.
- **Consent & deepfakes:** never put a real photo of someone else into AI without their "yes". Never use classmates at all. Deepfakes can be mean, can be used to trick people, and can be against the law.
- **Privacy:** never put your full name, address, school, passwords or secret codes into AI.
- **Be honest:** make your own work. Say when you used AI: #MadeWithAI.

```solo
Ask the student to try the bias test in Gemini: type "a scientist", look, then type "a group of different scientists from around the world". What changed?
```

## 3. Hands-on build — My AI Safety Pact (30 min · 1-on-1: 20 min)
**Part A — Fact-check mission (10 min · 1-on-1: 7 min):** take an answer from Gemini about a school topic (science or history). Pick one fact. Check it with the Two-Source Rule. Write: fact → source 1 → source 2 → true or false?
**Checkpoint 1 (+20 XP).**

**Part B — Safety Pact (20 min · 1-on-1: 13 min):** write a personal **AI Safety Pact**:

| Promise | My promise in my own words | What I'd do if… |
|---|---|---|
| Age rules | | …a friend says "just lie about your age" |
| Consent | | …someone asks me to change a classmate's photo |
| Privacy | | …an app asks for my home address |
| Honesty | | …I used AI for part of my homework |
| Checking | | …the AI gives me a fact for a project |

**Checkpoint 2 (+20 XP):** 5 promises + 5 "what I'd do if" answers, signed.
Decorate it if you like. It goes in your portfolio. A parent can sign it too.

```group
Part A in pairs, Part B alone.
```

```solo
Let the student talk first and write second. Ask "What would you really do?" for each row. If writing is slow, you write what they say, then they sign it.
```

## 4. Game / challenge — Safe or Sorry? (20 min · 1-on-1: 15 min)
Read each card. Choose **Safe**, **Sorry** or **Depends**. Give a reason using one of today's words.
1. "Make a funny AI picture of our teacher as a superhero and post it." → Sorry (no consent)
2. "Use AI to make a logo for our class bake sale and say it's AI-made." → Safe
3. "Copy an AI answer about the Moon into my report without checking." → Sorry (could be a hallucination)
4. "My 12-year-old cousin wants to make an account on an 18+ AI app." → Sorry (ToS). Use a school tool or ask an adult.
5. "Ask AI for a picture of 'a doctor' and all the doctors look the same." → Depends. Notice the bias, then ask for more kinds of people.
6. "Put my friend's voice into an AI voice copier as a prank." → Sorry (deepfake, no consent)
7. "Type my password into a chatbot so it can help me log in." → Sorry (privacy)
8. "Make a fake 5-star review for our pretend store." → Sorry (not honest)

```group
Houses get 2 minutes per card, then a quick reveal. +15 XP to the house with the best reasons. +5 to every house that finishes.
```

```solo
**Hot seat.** Read each card. The student answers in 10 seconds, then gives the reason. After 4 cards, swap: the student reads, and YOU answer. Get one wrong on purpose and see if they catch you. 15 XP for playing, +5 for catching you.
```

## 5. Share-out — Pact circle (10 min · 1-on-1: 5 min)
Read one promise out loud. Everyone with a signed pact gets the **🛡️ Safety Pact** badge. Add it in the XP tracker.

```solo
The student reads their favorite promise to you. Take a photo of the pact (no faces) for their portfolio. Award the badge right away.
```

## 6. Exit ticket (5 min)
1. What is a hallucination? How do you catch one?
2. Give one example of AI bias. Where does it come from?
3. Why do we never put a classmate's photo into an AI tool?

```solo
Ask out loud. Write down any rule they forget. Start next lesson with it.
```

In class, students use school-managed accounts or watch teacher-led demos for age-restricted tools. Your child has written an AI Safety Pact — ask them about it! If your child is 13+ and you are happy for them to use a specific tool with their own account, please sign the attached permission form.

## Differentiation
- **Extra help:** give the 'Safe or Sorry?' cards with little pictures. Give a sentence starter: "This is Sorry because… (consent / privacy / ToS)". Start 2 of the promises for them.
- **Extra challenge:** find a real news story about a deepfake or an AI mistake (side quest +25 XP). Tell what we can learn from it in 60 seconds.
- **1-on-1 tip:** this lesson is a great time to talk about the apps the student already uses at home. Keep it calm and curious, not scary.
