## Lesson objectives
Students will be able to…
- Explain why AI **hallucinates** (it predicts what sounds right, not what is true).
- Use the **Two-Source Rule** to verify a fact.
- Recognise **bias** in AI images and describe where it comes from.
- Explain age rules (**ToS**), **consent** and why **deepfakes** cause harm, and commit to a personal Safety Pact.

## Vocabulary today
hallucination · bias · Terms of Service (ToS) · consent · deepfake · privacy · disclosure

---

## 1. Warm-up — Two Truths and a Hallucination (10 min)
Show three AI-written "facts" about an animal (e.g. octopuses). One is invented. Houses vote, then must name **how** they could check. Reveal with a trusted source on screen.

```teacher
The AI wrote all three with the same confidence. It didn't lie on purpose — it predicted words that SOUND like an octopus fact. That's called a hallucination. Our job as creators is to check.
```

## 2. Mini-lesson — Three ways AI goes wrong (15 min)
Show **Visual 1 (Mistake Detective poster)**:
1. **Hallucination** — confident but false. *Fix:* Two-Source Rule (check 2 trusted sources that agree).
2. **Bias** — show the 4 images you generated ("a scientist", "a CEO"…). Ask: "What do you notice about who appears?" Explain: if the training data shows mostly one kind of person, the AI repeats that pattern. *Fix:* notice it, prompt for variety, and say something.
3. **Out-of-date info** — models are trained up to a date; they may not know recent news. *Fix:* check the date of your source.

Then **the rules of the road** (**Visual 2**):
- **Age rules (ToS):** many tools are 13+ or 18+. These laws (like COPPA) protect kids' data. In class we use school accounts or teacher-led demos.
- **Consent & deepfakes:** never upload or edit a real photo of someone else without their permission — and in this class, never classmates at all. Deepfakes can embarrass, bully, or scam people, and can be illegal.
- **Privacy:** no full names, addresses, school name, passwords, or API keys into AI.
- **Copyright & honesty:** make original work; say when you used AI (#MadeWithAI).

## 3. Hands-on build — My AI Safety Pact (30 min)
Part A (10 min) — **Fact-check mission:** each pair gets an AI answer about a topic from another class (science/history). Find one claim and verify it using the Two-Source Rule. Record: claim → source 1 → source 2 → verdict.
**Checkpoint 1 (+20 XP).**

Part B (20 min) — Students write their personal **AI Safety Pact** using the template:

| Promise area | My promise in my own words | What I'd do if… |
|---|---|---|
| Age rules | | …a friend says "just lie about your age" |
| Consent | | …someone asks me to edit a classmate's photo |
| Privacy | | …an app asks for my address |
| Honesty | | …I used AI for part of my homework |
| Checking | | …the AI gives me a fact for a project |

**Checkpoint 2 (+20 XP):** 5 promises + 5 "what I'd do if" answers, signed.
Students may decorate it; it goes in their portfolio. Optional parent signature.

## 4. Game / challenge — Safe or Sorry? (20 min)
Houses get scenario cards, decide **Safe / Sorry / Depends**, and justify with a vocabulary word. 2 minutes per card, then quick reveal and discussion:
1. "Make a funny AI picture of our teacher as a superhero and post it." → Sorry (no consent).
2. "Use AI to make a logo for our class bake sale and label it AI-made." → Safe.
3. "Copy an AI answer about the Moon into my report without checking." → Sorry (hallucination risk).
4. "My 12-year-old cousin wants to make a ChatGPT account." → Sorry (ToS) — use a school tool or ask an adult.
5. "Ask AI to make a picture of 'a doctor' and all results look the same." → Depends — notice the bias, prompt for variety.
6. "Put my friend's voice into an AI voice cloner as a prank." → Sorry (deepfake, consent).
7. "Paste my password into a chatbot so it can help me log in." → Sorry (privacy).
8. "Make a fake 5-star review for our pretend store." → Sorry (dishonest advertising).

Scoring: +15 XP to the house with the most well-justified answers; +5 for every house that finishes.

## 5. Share-out — Pact circle (10 min)
Volunteers read one promise aloud. Teacher awards the **🛡️ Safety Pact** badge to everyone with a completed, signed pact (record in the XP tracker).

## 6. Exit ticket (5 min)
1. What is a hallucination, and how do you catch one?
2. Give one example of AI bias and where it comes from.
3. Why must we never put a classmate's photo into an AI tool?

> Dear families, this year our class is learning to use AI tools creatively and safely. Many AI apps require users to be 13+ (with parent permission) or 18+. In class, students use school-managed accounts or watch teacher-led demos for age-restricted tools. Your child has written an AI Safety Pact — ask them about it! If your child is 13+ and you are happy for them to use a specific tool with their own account, please sign the attached permission form.

## Differentiation
- **Support:** scenario cards with picture icons; sentence frame "This is Sorry because… (consent/privacy/ToS)…"; pact template with 2 promises started.
- **Extension:** research a real news story about a deepfake or AI hallucination (side quest +25 XP) and present the lesson learned in 60 seconds.
