## Lesson objectives
By the end, the student can…
- Say what **AI**, **model**, **training data** and **prediction** mean, in their own words.
- Train a tiny AI that sorts pictures, and say why it sometimes gets it wrong.
- Explain why more kinds of examples make a better AI.

## Words for today
- **AI** — a computer program that learns from examples.
- **Model** — the "brain" the AI builds from those examples.
- **Training data** — the examples the AI learns from.
- **Pattern** — something that shows up again and again (cats have pointy ears).
- **Prediction** — the AI's best guess.
- **Bias** (sneak peek) — when the examples are not fair or not mixed enough.

## 1. Warm-up — "Can a computer read your doodle?" (10 min · 1-on-1: 5 min)
1. Open **Quick, Draw!** and play 2 rounds.
2. Ask: *"How did it guess? Where did it learn that?"*
3. Write down the guesses. Don't fix them yet.

```teacher
Welcome to the AI Creators Guild! This year you won't just use AI. You will learn how it works, and you will build real things with it. First question: how did that game know your squiggle was a cat?
```

```group
Play in pairs (6 doodles each). Pairs write their guess on a whiteboard. Collect 3 or 4 guesses on the board.
```

```solo
Take turns. You draw one, the student draws one. Race to see whose doodle the AI guesses first. Then ask the student for their best guess about how it works. Write it down. You'll come back to it in the share-out.
```

## 2. Mini-lesson — Input → Pattern → Prediction (15 min · 1-on-1: 10 min)
Show **Visual 1**. Walk through it with the Quick, Draw! game:
- **Training data:** millions of doodles. People already put a name on each one ("cat", "bike").
- **Model:** the computer found patterns. Cats have pointy ears and whiskers.
- **Prediction:** your new doodle gets checked against those patterns. "I'm 87% sure it's a cat."

Say this line again and again: **"AI doesn't know. It guesses from patterns."**

```teacher
Imagine every apple the AI ever saw was a red circle with a stem. Now I draw a green apple with no stem. What will it guess? Maybe "ball" or "circle". The examples shape the AI.
```

Quick check, thumbs up or down:
- "AI understands things like a person does." 👎
- "Better examples give better guesses." 👍

```solo
Skip the thumbs. Ask the student to draw the 3 boxes (Input → Pattern → Prediction) from memory. Let them fill in the cat example. Fix anything they mix up.
```

## 3. Hands-on build — Train your own AI (35 min · 1-on-1: 25 min)
Open **Teachable Machine → Image Project → Standard**.
1. **Class 1: "Pencil."** Hold up a pencil. Record about 50 webcam pictures. Turn it in different ways.
2. **Class 2: "Eraser."** Do the same.
3. **Class 3: "Nothing."** Just the empty background.
4. Click **Train Model**. Then test it in the Preview box.
5. Fill in the **Training Data Detective** sheet:

| Test | What I showed | What the AI guessed | How sure (%) | Right? Why or why not? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pencil, normal | | | |
| 2 | Pencil, upside down | | | |
| 3 | A pen (it never saw one) | | | |
| 4 | Pencil in low light | | | |

**Checkpoint 1 (+20 XP):** AI trained and 4 tests written down.
**Checkpoint 2 (+20 XP):** one sentence: "Our AI was wrong when ___ because the training data ___."

```group
Work in pairs. One holds the object, one clicks. Swap halfway.
```

```solo
The student runs the mouse and you hold the objects. Then swap. Let the student pick a 4th test of their own idea. Ask "What do you think will happen?" before every test.
```

> 🛡️ **Safety moment:** We point the webcam at THINGS, not faces. A picture of a face is private. We never collect other people's faces without asking.

## 4. Game / challenge — "Break the Model" (15 min · 1-on-1: 10 min)
Goal: make the AI **sure and wrong**. That means 90% or more on the wrong answer. Then explain the trick in one sentence. Use the words *training data*.

Tricks that often work: a new thing with the same shape, a new background, darker light, or hiding half the object.

```group
Houses compete. Each pair gets 5 minutes. 15 house XP for each pair with a good reason. +15 for the most creative trick.
```

```solo
**You vs. Bolt.** You and the student each get 3 tries to break the model. One point for each "sure but wrong" result. One more point for a good "because the training data…" sentence. Let the student win fair and square. They get 15 XP for playing and +15 if they beat you.
```

## 5. Share-out — "Mini TED talk" (10 min · 1-on-1: 5 min)
Present the best trick in 60 seconds. What did you show? What did the AI guess? Why?
Connect it back to the drawing. Then teach the word **bias**:

```teacher
Our AI only saw pencils on one white table. That is a one-sided set of examples. We call that bias. Bias means the AI learned from examples that were not mixed enough.
```

```group
3 pairs present. The class links each trick to the drawing.
```

```solo
The student explains their best trick to you like you are a new student who missed the class. Ask one "why" question. Look back at their warm-up guess together. Were they right?
```

## 6. Exit ticket (5 min)
1. In your own words, what is **training data**?
2. Why did your AI make a mistake? Use the word *pattern*.
3. What is one question you have about AI?

```solo
Ask the questions out loud and let the student answer by talking. Write down anything they're unsure about. Start the next lesson with it.
```

## Differentiation
- **Extra help:** fill in the first row of the sheet for them. Read the steps out loud together. Give a sentence starter: "The AI found the pattern ___."
- **Extra challenge:** add a 4th class. Does the AI get less sure? Try an Audio Project (claps vs. snaps).
- **1-on-1 tip:** go at the student's speed. If they get it fast, jump to the extra challenge. If not, do just 2 classes.
