## Lesson objectives
Students will be able to…
- Use **background removal, inpainting/generative fill, outpainting/expand, upscaling** on a product photo.
- Explain the difference between an **honest edit** and a **misleading edit**.
- Explain why editing photos of real people without consent is harmful (**deepfakes**) and add disclosure labels.

## Vocabulary today
inpainting · generative fill · outpainting / expand · upscaling · background removal · content credentials · deepfake · consent

---

## 1. Warm-up — Real or Edited? (10 min)
Show 6 images (objects/landscapes you edited). Houses vote real or edited and point to the clue (shadows, edges, repeated patterns). Reveal. Ask: "If you can't tell, how would anyone online know?"

## 2. Mini-lesson — The four AI edits (15 min)
Live demo on ONE product photo (e.g. a sneaker) — show **Visual 1**:
1. **Background removal** — cut out the subject (product shots, stickers).
2. **Inpainting / generative fill** — select an area and describe what goes there ("replace the coffee stain with nothing", "add a small potted plant").
3. **Outpainting / expand** — make the image wider/taller and let AI fill the edges (turn 1:1 into a 16:9 banner).
4. **Upscaling** — make a small image bigger and sharper.

**Honest vs. misleading** (**Visual 2**):
- ✅ Clean the background, fix lighting, place the product in a new scene **and label it**.
- ❌ Make the product look bigger/better than it is, fake "results", fake customers, edit a real person's face or body.

**Consent & deepfakes:** a few clicks can put someone's face in a fake situation. Even "jokes" can humiliate, spread forever, and in many places break the law. **Rule:** we edit objects, places, AI-made characters — never real people. Content Credentials / watermarks (like SynthID) are how platforms try to label AI images.

## 3. Hands-on build — Product Photo Glow-Up (35 min)
In pairs, photograph one object (startup product stand-in) at the photo corner. Then:

| Step | Technique | Result saved as |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Remove background | `product-cutout.png` |
| 2 | Place it in a new AI-generated scene that fits the brand (Image Prompt Formula) | `product-scene.png` |
| 3 | Generative fill: add or remove one detail | `product-fill.png` |
| 4 | Expand to 16:9 banner with negative space for text | `product-banner.png` |
| 5 | Add "AI-edited" label | — |

**Checkpoint 1 (+20 XP):** steps 1–2 done.
**Checkpoint 2 (+20 XP):** steps 3–5 done + one sentence: "This edit is honest because…"

> Teacher-led alternative: if school AI editing is off, the teacher does steps 2–4 on the projector with students dictating prompts; students do step 1 in Canva's free background remover or Photopea by hand.

## 4. Game / challenge — Honest or Misleading? Ad Court (15 min)
Houses get ad scenario cards and act as judges: **Honest / Misleading / Needs a label**. Justify with vocabulary.
1. Burger photo edited so it's 3× bigger than the real one. → Misleading
2. Water bottle cut out and placed on a mountain scene, labelled "AI-edited background". → Honest
3. Before/after skin-cream photo made with AI. → Misleading (fake results)
4. Sneaker colors brightened slightly to match real life under good light. → Honest
5. An AI-made "happy customer" photo with a fake quote. → Misleading (fake testimonial)
6. A banner expanded with outpainting to fit a website. → Honest (label it)

Most correct + best reasons: +15 XP; all finishers +5.

## 5. Share-out — Before/After wall (10 min)
Pairs post their before/after pairs. Classmates guess which technique was used for each change.

## 6. Exit ticket (5 min)
1. Match: inpainting / outpainting / upscaling / background removal → definition.
2. Give one honest and one misleading edit.
3. Why do we never edit photos of real people in this class?
