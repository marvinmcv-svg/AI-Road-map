## Lesson objectives
By the end, the student can…
- Say in 2 or 3 sentences how image AI turns **noise** into a picture.
- Write a prompt with all 6 parts of the **Image Prompt Formula**.
- Look at two pictures and say which words in the prompt changed them.

## Words for today
- **Noise** — fuzzy static, like a TV with no signal.
- **Text-to-image** — you type words, the AI makes a picture.
- **Style** — the look of a picture (cartoon, watercolor, photo).
- **Shape (aspect ratio)** — how wide or tall the picture is. Square is 1:1.

## 1. Warm-up — Recall Raid + the new quest (10 min · 1-on-1: 5 min)
Quick recall: *What is training data? What is a hallucination? Name the 5 Prompt Recipe parts.*
Then read the quest card out loud, with drama:

```teacher
The Guild has a new client! A brand new company needs a whole new look. Memes, product photos, a logo, ads and study cards. For the next 9 lessons, YOU are its design team. You'll make up the company in lesson 5. But first, you need to learn your new tool: Gemini's picture maker.
```

```solo
Hand the student the quest card and let THEM read it out loud like a movie trailer. Ask: "What kind of company would you love to design for?" Write their ideas down for lesson 5.
```

## 2. Mini-lesson — Noise to picture (15 min · 1-on-1: 10 min)
1. Show **Visual 1**: 5 frames. Static → blurry blobs → shapes → a clear picture.
2. Explain: picture AIs learned from billions of pictures that had words with them. They learned to **clean away noise**, step by step. When you type a prompt, Gemini starts with random static. Then it cleans it, little by little, into a picture that matches your words.
3. Think of it like fog: "A picture is hiding in the fog. Your words tell the AI what to look for. The fog clears a bit at a time."
4. Why it matters:
   - **Every picture starts from new random static.** That's why the same prompt gives a new picture each time.
   - Words the AI saw a lot (like "cat") work better than fuzzy words (like "nice thing").
5. Show the **Image Prompt Formula** (**Visual 2**):

| Part | Ask yourself | Word ideas |
|---|---|---|
| **Subject** | Who or what? Doing what? | a red fox reading a map |
| **Setting** | Where? When? | in a snowy forest at sunrise |
| **Style** | What art look? | pixel art, watercolor, 3D clay, cartoon, photo |
| **Camera** | How close? From where? | close-up, far away, from above, in the middle |
| **Light & color** | What mood? | golden sunset, neon, soft pastel, bright and bold |
| **Shape** | What will it be used for? | square 1:1, phone 9:16, wide 16:9 |

Example: *"A red fox reading a paper map (subject) in a snowy forest at sunrise (setting), watercolor painting (style), seen from far away, fox on the left side (camera), soft golden light (light & color), wide 16:9 (shape)."*

```solo
Ask the student to point to each part in the example and name it. Then cover the table and see how many parts they can remember.
```

## 3. Hands-on build — Formula ladder (35 min · 1-on-1: 25 min)
All pictures are made in **Gemini**.
1. **Copy and run** the fox prompt. Save the picture.
2. **Change ONE part at a time.** Guess what will happen before you press Enter:
   - Change the **Style** → what changed?
   - Change the **Camera** → what changed?
   - Change the **Light & color** → what changed?
3. **Your own picture:** write a full 6-part prompt for a scene you love (a sport, a hobby, a food, a fantasy world).
4. Write it all in the log:

| # | Part I changed | My guess | What really happened |
|---|---|---|---|

**Checkpoint 1 (+20 XP):** 3 one-change tests written down with guesses.
**Checkpoint 2 (+20 XP):** the student's own 6-part picture saved in their folder with the label *#MadeWithAI*.

```group
Everyone works alone. Walk around and ask: "What do you think will change?"
```

```solo
Sit side by side. Let the student type every prompt. Your job is to ask "What's your guess?" before each one. If they love their own picture, let them make 2 or 3 more tries. Count each one.
```

> 🛡️ **Copying moment:** We don't type "in the style of [a real living artist]". We don't use characters like Mario. Describe the look instead: "thick outlines, bright flat colors, chunky shapes." Artists should get to own their style.

## 4. Game / challenge — Reverse Engineer (15 min · 1-on-1: 10 min)
Show 3 AI pictures. For each one, you have 2 minutes to guess the 6 formula parts that made it. Points for each part you get right (style, camera, light word, shape).

```group
Houses play. Most points: +15 XP. Everyone who finishes: +5.
```

```solo
**Student vs. Bolt.** You both write your guess for each picture. Then the student types their guess into Gemini. Whose picture came out closer? Next round, the student makes a picture and YOU guess its prompt. +15 XP for the winner, +5 for finishing.
```

## 5. Share-out — Formula gallery (10 min · 1-on-1: 5 min)
Look at the student's own picture. Use the feedback lines: *"Your best formula part is… / One part to make clearer is…"*

```group
Gallery walk. Students leave feedback cards on each other's pictures.
```

```solo
The student tells you their favorite part of their picture. You give one "best part" and one "make it clearer". Let them try that one fix right now.
```

## 6. Exit ticket (5 min)
1. How does image AI make a picture? Use the word *noise*.
2. Why can the same prompt give two different pictures?
3. Which formula part made the biggest change today?

```solo
Ask out loud. If they get stuck on #1, show Visual 1 again and let them tell the story of the 5 frames.
```

## Visuals to show
**Visual 1 — Noise to image filmstrip.**
```prompt
An educational filmstrip with 5 frames in a row, 16:9, titled "FROM NOISE TO PICTURE". Frame 1: pure colorful TV static labelled "Step 1: noise". Frame 2: blurry color blobs "Step 10". Frame 3: rough shapes of a fox and trees "Step 25". Frame 4: clearer fox in a snowy forest "Step 40". Frame 5: crisp watercolor fox reading a map "Done!". Arrows between frames, clean white background, bold rounded labels, middle school friendly.
```

**Visual 2 — Image Prompt Formula poster.**
```prompt
A classroom poster titled "THE IMAGE PROMPT FORMULA", portrait 3:4. Six colorful stacked blocks like a burger, each with an icon and label: SUBJECT (star), SETTING (map pin), STYLE (paint palette), CAMERA (camera frame), LIGHT & COLOR (sun), SHAPE (rectangle shapes 1:1, 9:16, 16:9). Each block has a tiny example in smaller text. Playful flat design, bright colors, very readable.
```

**Visual 3 — One prompt, three styles (before/after comparison).**
```prompt
A three-panel comparison, 16:9, same subject in each panel: a red fox reading a paper map in a snowy forest. Panel 1 labelled "PIXEL ART" in 16-bit pixel style. Panel 2 labelled "WATERCOLOR" soft painted style. Panel 3 labelled "3D CLAY" claymation style. Same composition in all three, fox on the left third. Clean white borders and bold labels.
```

## Differentiation
- **Extra help:** give word lists for each formula part. The student picks one word from each list. Do the first change together.
- **Extra challenge:** make the same subject in 4 styles and put them in a grid. Which words did Gemini get best?
- **1-on-1 tip:** use the student's hobby or favorite animal for every example today.
