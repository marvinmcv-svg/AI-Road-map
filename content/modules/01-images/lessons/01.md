## Lesson objectives
Students will be able to…
- Explain in 2–3 sentences how a **diffusion model** turns **noise** into an image.
- Write a prompt with all six parts of the **Image Prompt Formula**.
- Compare outputs and explain which prompt words changed the image.

## Vocabulary today
diffusion model · noise · text-to-image · style · aspect ratio

---

## 1. Warm-up — Recall Raid + Quest launch (10 min)
Recall Raid (whiteboards): *What is training data? What's a hallucination? Name the 5 Prompt Recipe parts.*
Then the quest card (read aloud dramatically):

```teacher
The Guild has a new client! A startup needs a whole visual identity — memes, product photos, a logo, ads and study guides. Over the next 9 lessons, YOU will be its design studio. You'll each invent your startup in lesson 5. First, you need to understand your new tool.
```

## 2. Mini-lesson — Noise to picture (15 min)
1. Show **Visual 1**: 5 frames from static → blurry blobs → shapes → clear image.
2. Explain: image AIs were trained on billions of images with captions. They learned how to **remove noise** step by step. When you prompt, the AI starts from random static and "cleans it" toward pictures that match your words.
3. Analogy: "A sculptor sees a statue inside the marble and chips away. The AI 'sees' your prompt inside the static."
4. Why it matters: **every generation starts from different random noise** → that's why the same prompt gives different images. And words the AI saw often in training (like "cat") work better than vague words ("nice thing").
5. Introduce the **Image Prompt Formula** (**Visual 2**):

| Part | Ask yourself | Word bank |
|---|---|---|
| **Subject** | Who/what is it? Doing what? | a red fox reading a map |
| **Setting** | Where/when? | in a snowy forest at dawn |
| **Style** | What art style? | pixel art, watercolor, 3D clay, flat vector, photo |
| **Composition** | How close? From where? | close-up, wide shot, bird's-eye view, centered |
| **Lighting/color** | What mood? | golden hour, neon, soft pastel, high contrast |
| **Format** | What shape/use? | square 1:1, phone story 9:16, slide 16:9 |

Worked example: *"A red fox reading a paper map (subject) in a snowy forest at dawn (setting), watercolor illustration (style), wide shot, fox on the left third (composition), soft golden light (lighting), 16:9 (format)."*

## 3. Hands-on build — Formula ladder (35 min)
1. **Copy & run** the worked example prompt (or teacher runs it). Save it. *(Worked example)*
2. **Change ONE part at a time** and predict before generating:
   - Change the **Style** → what changed?
   - Change the **Composition** → what changed?
   - Change the **Lighting** → what changed?
3. **Your own image:** write a full six-part prompt for a scene you love (sport, hobby, food, fantasy).
4. Log everything:

| # | Part I changed | My prediction | What actually happened |
|---|---|---|---|

**Checkpoint 1 (+20 XP):** 3 single-change experiments logged with predictions.
**Checkpoint 2 (+20 XP):** original six-part image saved to the class Canva folder with the label *#MadeWithAI*.

> 🛡️ **Copyright moment:** We don't write "in the style of [a living artist]" or use characters like Mario. Describe the look instead: "bold outlines, bright flat colors, chunky shapes." Artists deserve control of their style.

## 4. Game / challenge — Reverse Engineer (15 min)
Show 3 AI images. For each, houses have 2 minutes to write the six formula parts that probably made it. Points for each part named correctly (style name, shot type, lighting word, aspect ratio). House with most points: +15 XP; all finishers +5.

## 5. Share-out — Formula gallery (10 min)
Gallery walk of original images. Feedback cards: *"Your strongest formula part is… / One part to make more specific is…"*

## 6. Exit ticket (5 min)
1. Explain how a diffusion model makes an image (use *noise*).
2. Why can the same prompt give two different images?
3. Which formula part made the biggest difference today?

```prompt
An educational filmstrip with 5 frames in a row, 16:9, titled "FROM NOISE TO PICTURE". Frame 1: pure colorful TV static labelled "Step 1: noise". Frame 2: blurry color blobs "Step 10". Frame 3: rough shapes of a fox and trees "Step 25". Frame 4: clearer fox in a snowy forest "Step 40". Frame 5: crisp watercolor fox reading a map "Done!". Arrows between frames, clean white background, bold rounded labels, middle school friendly.
```

```prompt
A classroom poster titled "THE IMAGE PROMPT FORMULA", portrait 3:4. Six colorful stacked blocks like a burger, each with an icon and label: SUBJECT (star), SETTING (map pin), STYLE (paint palette), COMPOSITION (camera frame), LIGHTING & COLOR (sun), FORMAT (rectangle shapes 1:1, 9:16, 16:9). ```prompt
```
```prompt
A three-panel comparison, 16:9, same subject in each panel: a red fox reading a paper map in a snowy forest. Panel 1 labelled "PIXEL ART" in 16-bit pixel style. ## Differentiation
```
- **Support:** fill-in-the-blank formula card with word banks; teacher pre-writes Subject + Setting, student picks the rest.
- **Extension:** test "negative" instructions ("no text, no people") and record when the AI ignores them; research what "seed" means in image generation.
