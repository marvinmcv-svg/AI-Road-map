## Lesson objectives
By the end, the student can…
- Write a **Brand Brief** (name, buyers, personality, mission).
- Name 5 logo types: **word logo, letter logo, icon, icon + name, badge**.
- Design a logo that still works when it's tiny, with colors picked on purpose.

## Words for today
- **Logo** — the small picture or name that shows who made something.
- **Brand** — how people see and feel about a company.
- **Personality words** — 3 words that say how the brand should feel (playful, calm, bold).
- **Scales well** — still looks good when tiny or huge.
- **Color meaning** — the feeling a color gives (blue feels calm).

## 1. Warm-up — Draw it from memory (10 min · 1-on-1: 5 min)
```teacher
A great logo still works when it is tiny, like an app icon. Today you will make one for your brand. First, let's see what you remember.
```

Quick recall: "Name the 4 AI edits from last lesson."
Then: "Draw a famous food or tech logo from memory in 30 seconds." (Just drawing. We never copy them into projects.) Ask: "Why could you remember it?" Simple shapes, few colors, seen everywhere. That's today's lesson.

```solo
You both draw the same famous logo from memory, then compare. Who got closer? Talk about what made it easy to remember.
```

## 2. Mini-lesson — What makes a logo work (15 min · 1-on-1: 10 min)
**Visual 1 — Logo types** (all made-up examples):
| Type | What it is | Good when… |
|---|---|---|
| Word logo (wordmark) | The name in a cool font | The name is short and easy to remember |
| Letter logo (lettermark) | Just the first letters | The name is long |
| Icon | Just a symbol | People already know the brand. App icons. |
| Icon + name (combination mark) | Symbol and name together | **New brands. This is what we'll make.** |
| Badge (emblem) | The name inside a shape | Clubs, schools, food brands |

**The 4 logo rules:** Simple · Works tiny AND huge · Easy to remember · Fits the brand's personality.

**What colors make people feel (Visual 2):** blue = calm and trust, green = nature and health, yellow or orange = fun and energy, red = exciting and fast, purple = creative and magic, black = fancy. It's a guide, not a law. Colors can mean different things in different places.

**Gemini tip:** Gemini is great for logo ideas and is pretty good at words. But always check the spelling, letter by letter. If a word keeps coming out wrong, make the icon in Gemini and type the name yourself in Google Slides or Canva.

## 3. Hands-on build — Brand Brief → Logo (40 min · 1-on-1: 28 min)
**Part A — Brand Brief (10 min · 1-on-1: 7 min):**
| Brand name | |
|---|---|
| What it sells | |
| Buyers (who? how old? what do they care about?) | |
| 3 personality words | like playful, Earth-friendly, bold |
| Mission in one sentence | We help ___ to ___ by ___. |
| Colors I'm thinking about, and why | |

**Checkpoint 1 (+20 XP):** brief finished.

**Part B — Ideas (15 min · 1-on-1: 11 min):** make 6 icon ideas in **Gemini**. Example prompt:
*"Simple flat logo icon of a water drop shaped like a leaf, 2 colors (teal and lime), thick round lines, in the middle of a white background, no words, simple enough to be an app icon, 1:1."*
Pick the best 2. Ask Gemini for one fix on each ("make it simpler", "thicker lines", "only 2 colors").

**Part C — Icon + name (15 min · 1-on-1: 10 min):** ask Gemini: "Put this icon next to the name '[Brand]' in a bold, friendly font." Check every letter. Make 3 versions: side by side, stacked, and icon only. Write down the color codes (like #1CB0F6) in a Google Slide or the Canva Brand Kit.

**Checkpoint 2 (+20 XP):** 3 logo versions saved.

```group
Part A in pairs (interview each other about the brand). Parts B and C alone.
```

```solo
For Part A, interview the student like a real client meeting: "Tell me about your company. Who buys it? How should it feel?" Write what they say into the brief. Then they run Parts B and C in Gemini.
```

> 🛡️ **Copying moment:** your logo must not look like a real brand. No swooshes, no bitten apples, no golden arches. Copycat logos can get companies in big trouble, and it's not fair to the designers who made them.

## 4. Game / challenge — Squint & Shrink (10 min · 1-on-1: 7 min)
Make the logo tiny (the size of a browser tab icon) and big. Look at it from 3 big steps away and squint. *Can you still tell what it is? Can you read the name when it's small?* Pass both = +15 XP. Fix it and test again = +5.

```group
A partner from another house does the test.
```

```solo
You do the test, then the student tests a logo YOU made in Gemini (make it a bit too busy on purpose). Can they spot what's wrong and tell Gemini how to fix it?
```

## 5. Share-out — Does it match the brief? (10 min · 1-on-1: 5 min)
Read your 3 personality words out loud. Show the logo. Rate it 1 to 5: "Does the logo feel like these words?" Give one idea to make it better.

```group
In groups of 3.
```

```solo
Rate it 1 to 5 together. Ask a family member or another teacher to guess the 3 personality words just from the logo. Did they get close?
```

## 6. Exit ticket (5 min)
1. Name 3 logo types.
2. What does "works tiny" mean for a logo?
3. Why did you pick your main brand color?

```solo
Ask out loud. For #3, they should use a feeling word.
```

## Differentiation
- **Extra help:** start with the Brand Brief half filled in from their lesson 2 notes. Make 3 icon ideas instead of 6.
- **Extra challenge:** make a dark-mode version and a one-color version (just black). Does it still work?
- **1-on-1 tip:** treat the student like a real client and a real designer at the same time. Ask "Do you love it?" If not, keep going.
