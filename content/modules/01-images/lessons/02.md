## Lesson objectives
By the end, the student can…
- Use 5 or more **camera shots** in prompts.
- Use the **rule of thirds** and pick the right **shape** for a job.
- Name 6 art **styles** and describe a style without copying an artist.

## Words for today
- **Camera shot** — how close the camera is, and where it looks from.
- **Rule of thirds** — split the picture into a 3 × 3 grid. Put the main thing on a line, not in the middle.
- **Empty space** — a calm, empty part of the picture. It leaves room for words.
- **Shape (aspect ratio)** — how wide or tall a picture is.
- **Style** — the art look.

## 1. Warm-up — Formula recall + "Where's the subject?" (10 min · 1-on-1: 5 min)
Name the 6 Image Prompt Formula parts. Then look at 3 photos or paintings. Point to where the main thing sits. Ask: "Why didn't the artist put it right in the middle?"

```solo
The student names the parts while you count on your fingers. Then they point at the photos. Let them take one photo of an object on the desk, then a second one with the object moved to the side. Which looks cooler?
```

## 2. Mini-lesson — The photographer's toolkit (15 min · 1-on-1: 10 min)
Show **Visual 1 (Shot chart)**. One subject (a skateboarding robot) in each shot:
- **Far away (wide shot)** — shows the place. Good for setting the scene.
- **Middle (medium shot)** — from the waist up. Good for action.
- **Close-up / super close-up** — shows feelings and tiny details. Good for products.
- **From above (bird's-eye view)** — like a bird looking down. Good for maps, food and desks.
- **Looking up (low angle)** — makes things look big and strong.

Show **Visual 2 (Rule of thirds)**. A 3 × 3 grid. Put the subject on a line or where two lines cross. **Empty space** leaves room for words. *That's going to matter a lot for posters later!*

**Pick the shape for the job:**
| Shape | Use it for |
|---|---|
| 1:1 (square) | Posts, profile pictures, logos |
| 9:16 (tall) | Phone stories and short videos |
| 16:9 (wide) | Slides, video thumbnails, website banners |
| 4:5 (a bit tall) | Posters |

**Style words:** pixel art, flat cartoon, watercolor, 3D clay, comic book, photo-real, paper cut-out. Remember: describe the look. Never write "in the style of [a living artist]".

```solo
Make it a quick game: you say a shot, the student holds up their hands like a camera frame and shows you how close it is.
```

## 3. Hands-on build — The Shot List (35 min · 1-on-1: 25 min)
Pick ONE subject (like "a tiny dragon baking cupcakes"). Plan a **4-picture shot list**, like a movie director. Make each picture in **Gemini**.

| Shot | Why | Prompt (all 6 parts) |
|---|---|---|
| Far away, 16:9 | Show the place | |
| Middle, 1:1 | Show the action | |
| Close-up, 4:5, subject on the right, empty space on the left | Poster with room for a title | |
| From above, 1:1 | The cupcakes laid out on a table | |

**Checkpoint 1 (+20 XP):** all 4 prompts written BEFORE making any picture (plan first!).
**Checkpoint 2 (+20 XP):** 4 pictures made and placed in a grid (Google Slides or Canva). Each one has a label: shot + shape.

Ask while they work: "Why that shot? How should the viewer feel?"

```solo
Let the student pick a subject they love. If a picture comes out wrong, show them how to ask Gemini for a fix in the same chat: "Same picture, but from above." That's a great habit.
```

## 4. Game / challenge — Style Spinner (15 min · 1-on-1: 10 min)
Spin for a **style + shot + shape** (like "watercolor + looking up + 9:16"). Write a prompt for the same subject that uses the spin. Make it in Gemini. Does it match the spin?

```group
Houses have 3 minutes per spin. You make one picture per house. The class votes: does it match? Match +15 XP. Everyone who finishes +5.
```

```solo
**Student vs. Bolt.** Each of you spins and writes a prompt. Make both pictures. The other person must guess the spin just by looking. Guess right = 1 point for both of you (good prompt + good eye). Play 3 rounds. +15 XP for playing, +5 for 2 or more points.
```

## 5. Share-out — Director's talk (10 min · 1-on-1: 5 min)
Explain ONE choice: "I used 'looking up' because I wanted the dragon to look like a hero."

```group
Pairs swap shot lists and explain one choice each.
```

```solo
The student gives you a "director's tour" of their 4 pictures. One sentence each: shot, shape and why.
```

## 6. Exit ticket (5 min)
1. Look at 3 small pictures. Name the shot for each one.
2. Which shape would you use for a phone story? For a slide?
3. What is empty space good for?
4. **Pick your company:** make up your Guild client, or pick one: water bottles that help the Earth, a dog-walking app, a bakery for robots, a plant-care kit, a skateboard repair shop, a snack box for studying. Write: a name idea + what it sells + who buys it. (You'll finish the Brand Brief in lesson 5.)

```solo
Ask 1 to 3 out loud. Spend most of the time on #4. Let the student get excited about their company. It's theirs for the whole module.
```

## Visuals to show
**Visual 1 — Shot type chart.**
```prompt
An educational chart, 16:9, titled "SHOT TYPES & CAMERA ANGLES". Five panels showing the same cute skateboarding robot character: "WIDE SHOT" robot small in a city skate park; "MEDIUM SHOT" robot from waist up; "CLOSE-UP" robot's face with a big grin; "BIRD'S-EYE VIEW" from directly above; "LOW ANGLE" looking up at the robot heroically against the sky. Consistent friendly 3D cartoon style, clear bold labels under each panel, white background.
```

**Visual 2 — Rule of thirds overlay.**
```prompt
A photo-style illustration of a lighthouse on a cliff at sunset, 16:9, with a clear 3x3 white grid overlaid. The lighthouse sits exactly on the right vertical line, the horizon on the lower horizontal line. Glowing dots mark the four intersection points. Large empty sky on the left labelled "NEGATIVE SPACE (room for text!)". Title at top: "RULE OF THIRDS". Clean, educational, bold labels.
```

**Visual 3 — Aspect ratio cheat sheet.**
```prompt
A simple infographic, 16:9, titled "PICK YOUR SHAPE". Four rectangles drawn to scale side by side: a square "1:1 feed post", a tall phone "9:16 story/reel", a wide screen "16:9 slide & thumbnail", a portrait "4:5 poster". Each contains a tiny icon of its use. Flat pastel colors, bold labels, white background.
```

## Differentiation
- **Extra help:** use the Shot Cards with little pictures to copy into prompts. Make 3 pictures instead of 4.
- **Extra challenge:** add camera words like *macro*, *blurry background* or *fisheye*. Which ones does Gemini understand? Look at an old painting and find its rule of thirds lines.
- **1-on-1 tip:** take real photos of a toy with your phone first. Then make the same shots in Gemini. Real and AI side by side really helps.
