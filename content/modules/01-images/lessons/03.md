## Lesson objectives
By the end, the student can…
- Name the parts of a meme: **picture, setup, twist, 'that's so me!', shape**.
- Make 2 of their own memes with Gemini pictures. No real people. No famous meme pictures.
- Use the **kindness rule**: joke about things that happen, not about people.

## Words for today
- **Meme** — a funny picture with a few words that people share.
- **Setup** — the everyday moment everyone knows ("Me on Monday morning").
- **Twist (punchline)** — the funny surprise.
- **Relatable** — people look at it and think "that's SO me!"
- **Viral** — when lots and lots of people share something.

## 1. Warm-up — Shot quiz + meme talk (10 min · 1-on-1: 5 min)
Show 3 quick pictures. Name the shot and shape. Then ask: "What makes something funny online?" Name meme TYPES (not exact memes): before and after, what I wanted vs. what I got, "Nobody: / Me:".

```solo
Ask the student to tell you about a funny meme type they've seen (no need to show it). Ask: "Why is it funny?"
```

## 2. Mini-lesson — The parts of a meme (12 min · 1-on-1: 8 min)
Show **Visual 1 (Meme parts)**:
1. **Picture** — easy to "get" in one second. Close-ups of big faces work best.
2. **Setup** — the moment everyone knows ("Me on Monday morning").
3. **Twist** — the funny surprise.
4. **'That's so me!'** — people see themselves in it.
5. **Shape** — square or 4:5, big bold words, strong colors, few words.

Why brands use memes: people share **feelings**, not ads. A good brand meme is about the buyer's life, not "buy our stuff".

**The kindness & copying rules:**
- ✅ Everyday moments, animals, objects, our own made-up characters.
- ❌ Classmates, teachers, real people, groups of people, famous people, other people's characters or meme pictures.
- Label it: a small *#MadeWithAI* in the corner.

## 3. Hands-on build — Brand Meme Lab (38 min · 1-on-1: 27 min)
1. Write 3 setups your company's buyers know. (Water bottle company: "When you forget your water bottle on the hottest day.")
2. Pick a meme type for each: *what I wanted vs. what I got*, *two-picture reaction*, *"POV:"*, *level up (bronze → gold)*.
3. Make the picture in **Gemini** with all 6 formula parts. Tip: **close-up, big silly face, plain background, 1:1**.
4. Add the words. Two ways:
   - Ask Gemini to add short words on top. Gemini is pretty good at words, but **check every letter**.
   - Or add the words yourself in Google Slides or Canva. Use big bold letters, white with a dark edge.

**Checkpoint 1 (+20 XP):** 3 setups and meme types planned.
**Checkpoint 2 (+20 XP):** 2 finished memes, labeled, saved in the folder.

```solo
Brainstorm the setups together, out loud, with silly voices. The best memes come from real life, so ask: "What's annoying about [their company's thing]?" Then the student makes them in Gemini.
```

> 🛡️ **Kindness moment:** ask about every meme: "Could anyone feel picked on by this?" If yes, change it.

## 4. Game / challenge — Caption Battle (15 min · 1-on-1: 10 min)
Show one Gemini picture (like a cat in a tiny chef hat staring at a burnt cake). Write the best caption in 3 minutes. Read the captions out loud with no names. Vote.

```group
Houses each write one caption. The class votes by clapping. Winner +15 XP. Every house with a kind caption that's easy to read +5.
```

```solo
**Student vs. Bolt.** Make 3 pictures in Gemini. For each one, you both write a caption on paper. Mix them up and read them to a family member or another teacher, who picks the funniest. No judge around? Each of you picks the other's best one. +15 XP for playing, +5 for each win.
```

## 5. Share-out — Meme wall (10 min · 1-on-1: 5 min)
Rate memes with 😂 / 🤔 / 💡. Ask: "Which ones felt like 'that's so me'? Why?"

```solo
The student shows their 2 memes. You react honestly with 😂, 🤔 or 💡 and say why. They pick their best meme for the Brand Kit.
```

## 6. Exit ticket (5 min)
1. Name 3 parts of a meme.
2. Why should you check every letter when AI writes words on a picture?
3. Is this OK? "A meme with a photo of the school principal." Say why.

```solo
Ask out loud. For #3, let them explain the rule in their own words.
```

## Differentiation
- **Extra help:** give 3 ready setups to choose from. Use one meme type only (what I wanted vs. what I got).
- **Extra challenge:** make a 3-picture meme story. Or make the same joke for two different buyers (kids vs. parents) and compare.
- **1-on-1 tip:** humor is personal. Let the student's sense of humor lead, as long as the kindness rule holds.
