## Lesson objectives
Students will be able to…
- Identify the parts of a meme: **template/image, setup, punchline, relatability, format**.
- Create 2 original memes with AI-generated images (no real people, no copyrighted templates).
- Apply the **kindness rule**: punch at situations, not people.

## Vocabulary today
meme template · caption · relatability · format · viral · disclosure

---

## 1. Warm-up — Shot quiz + meme talk (10 min)
3 quick images → name the shot type and ratio. Then: "What makes something funny online? Shout out examples of meme FORMATS (not specific memes): before/after, expectation vs. reality, 'nobody: / me:'…"

## 2. Mini-lesson — Meme anatomy (12 min)
Show **Visual 1 (Meme anatomy)**:
1. **Image/template** — instantly readable picture (close-ups of expressions work best).
2. **Setup** — the situation everyone knows ("Me on Monday morning").
3. **Punchline** — the twist.
4. **Relatability** — the audience thinks "that's SO me".
5. **Format** — square or 4:5, big bold text, high contrast, few words.

Why memes matter for brands: they spread because people **share feelings**, not ads. Good brand memes are about the customer's life, not "buy our stuff".

**The kindness & copyright rules:**
- ✅ Situations, animals, objects, our own characters.
- ❌ Classmates, teachers, real people, groups of people, celebrities, copyrighted characters/templates.
- Label it: small *#MadeWithAI* in the corner.

## 3. Hands-on build — Brand Meme Lab (38 min)
1. Write 3 setups your startup's customers relate to (e.g. eco-bottle brand: "When you forget your water bottle on the hottest day").
2. For each, choose a meme format: *expectation vs. reality*, *two-panel reaction*, *"POV:"*, *level-up (bronze → gold)*.
3. Generate the image(s) with a full formula prompt — tip: **close-up, exaggerated expression, plain background, 1:1**.
4. Add text in **Canva** (not in the AI image — AI text is often garbled!). Use bold sans-serif, white text with dark outline or a solid bar.

**Checkpoint 1 (+20 XP):** 3 setups + formats planned.
**Checkpoint 2 (+20 XP):** 2 finished memes, labelled, saved to the class folder.

> 🛡️ **Ethics moment:** Ask each student: "Could anyone feel targeted by this?" If yes, rework it.

## 4. Game / challenge — Caption Battle (15 min)
Teacher shows one AI image (e.g. a cat in a tiny chef hat staring at a burnt cake). Houses write the best caption in 3 minutes. Anonymous captions are read aloud; class votes by applause meter or hand count. Winner +15 XP; every house with a kind, on-image-readable caption +5.

## 5. Share-out — Meme wall (10 min)
Gallery walk: students place 😂 / 🤔 / 💡 stickers. Discuss: "Which ones were relatable? Why?"

## 6. Exit ticket (5 min)
1. Name 3 parts of a meme.
2. Why should brand text be added in Canva instead of inside the AI image?
3. Is this OK? "A meme using a photo of the school principal." Explain.

## Visuals to show
**Visual 1 — Meme anatomy diagram.**
```prompt
An annotated diagram of a two-panel meme, 4:5. Top panel: a fluffy orange cat wearing a tiny chef hat proudly holding a perfect cake, labelled "EXPECTATION". Bottom panel: the same cat looking shocked at a burnt smoking cake, labelled "REALITY". Callout arrows around it pointing to: "IMAGE: clear expression", "SETUP: a situation everyone knows", "PUNCHLINE: the twist", "BIG BOLD TEXT", "#MadeWithAI label". Clean infographic style, white margins, bold arrows.
```

**Visual 2 — Kind vs. unkind meme rule.**
```prompt
A simple two-column poster, 16:9, titled "MEMES: PUNCH AT SITUATIONS, NOT PEOPLE". Left column with a green check: icons of a rainy Monday, a burnt toast, a sleepy cat, a cartoon robot. Right column with a red X: silhouette of a classmate, a camera taking someone's photo, a famous-person star, a copyright symbol. Flat bold icons, clear labels, friendly colors.
```

**Visual 3 — Meme format menu.**
```prompt
A colorful menu board graphic titled "MEME FORMAT MENU", 16:9, like a café chalkboard. Four items with small sketches: "Expectation vs Reality" (two panels), "POV:" (first-person view sketch), "Level Up" (bronze, silver, gold trophy steps), "Two-Panel Reaction" (calm face then surprised face). Chalk-style lettering, playful doodles, no real people or brands.
```

## Differentiation
- **Support:** setup sentence starters ("When you…", "POV: you just…"); pre-made Canva meme layouts.
- **Extension:** design a reusable **brand mascot meme template** with consistent character; write a 3-meme "content calendar" for a week.
