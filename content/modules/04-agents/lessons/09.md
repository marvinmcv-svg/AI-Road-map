## Lesson objectives
By the end, the student can…
- Run their helper on a **test set** it has never seen.
- Fill in a scorecard: **accuracy**, **safety**, **draft quality**, **time saved**.
- Change one thing and measure again to prove it got better.

## Words for today
- **Test set** — messages you keep for testing only, with the right answers written down.
- **Scorecard** — a few numbers that show how good something is.
- **Edit rate** — how often a human had to change the AI's draft.
- **Time saved** — (minutes by hand − minutes with the helper) × how often.
- **Baseline** — the first score, before you make changes.

## 1. Warm-up — Guess your score (10 min · 1-on-1: 5 min)
```teacher
How do you know your helper really works? You test it, and count. Today you are the scientist. Guess first, then we check.
```

How many of 30 test messages will your helper sort right? How many attacks will it block? Write your guesses.

```solo
You both guess the student's score. Closest guess gets bragging rights.
```

## 2. Mini-lesson — The Helper Scorecard (10 min · 1-on-1: 7 min)
Show **Visual 1**:
| Score | How to measure | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| 🎯 **Accuracy** | Right categories ÷ all messages | 85% or more |
| 🛡️ **Safety** | Attacks handled safely ÷ all attacks | 100% |
| ✏️ **Draft quality** | Edit rate: drafts you had to change ÷ all drafts | Lower is better |
| ⏱️ **Time saved** | (Minutes by hand − minutes with helper) × times per week | As high as possible |

**Baseline first.** Score it before changing anything. Then change ONE thing and score again. If you change 3 things at once, you won't know which one helped.

## 3. Hands-on build — Measure and improve (45 min · 1-on-1: 31 min)
1. Run your helper on the whole test set. Don't fix anything yet!
2. Fill in the scorecard: this is your **baseline**.
3. Paste the misses into Claude: "Here are the messages my helper got wrong: [paste]. What patterns do you see?" Then decide yourself.
4. Change ONE thing (a category meaning, a new example, a guardrail).
5. Run it again and fill in the scorecard. Better, worse or the same?
6. Repeat once more if time allows.
7. Work out **time saved** per week using your warm-up numbers from lessons 1 and 4.

**Checkpoint 1 (+20 XP):** baseline scorecard filled in.
**Checkpoint 2 (+20 XP):** one change made and measured again, with time saved worked out.

```group
Pairs check each other's scoring for fairness.
```

```solo
Be the strict grader. For draft quality, you and the student each mark drafts that need edits on your own, then compare. Agreeing on what "good" means is half the work.
```

> 🛡️ **Honest numbers moment:** don't change the answer key to make the score look better. A real score you can trust beats a perfect score you can't.

## 4. Game / challenge — One Fix Only (10 min · 1-on-1: 7 min)
You can make just ONE more change before Demo Day. Which one helps users most? Defend your choice in 30 seconds.

```group
Class votes on the best-defended choice: +15 XP. Everyone: +5.
```

```solo
**Student vs. Bolt.** You both pick the one fix for their helper, secretly. Reveal and debate. +15 XP for playing, +5 for a strong argument.
```

## 5. Share-out — Before and after (10 min · 1-on-1: 5 min)
Show baseline vs. final scorecard. Which change made the biggest difference?

```solo
The student presents their scorecard like a sports stats report.
```

## 6. Exit ticket (5 min)
1. What's a test set, and why keep it separate?
2. What was your baseline accuracy, and your best?
3. Why change only one thing at a time?

```solo
Ask out loud. Save the scorecard for the Boss demo.
```
