## Lesson objectives
By the end, the student can…
- Explain the difference between a **chatbot**, an **automation** and an **AI agent**.
- Map a workflow with 4 parts: **trigger → steps → tools → check**.
- Pick the 3 store jobs that are best to hand to a helper, and say why.

## Words for today
- **Workflow** — the steps you take to get a job done, in order.
- **Automation** — a computer doing the same steps by itself, the same way every time.
- **Trigger** — the thing that starts the workflow ("a new order comes in", "every Monday at 9").
- **AI agent** — an AI helper that can decide what to do next and use tools to do it.
- **Tool** (for an agent) — something the AI can use: email, a sheet, a calendar, a search.
- **Human check** — a person looks before anything important happens.

## 1. Warm-up — The boring list (10 min · 1-on-1: 5 min)
List every boring job you do again and again (school, work or home). Circle the one you'd love a robot to do.

```teacher
Your store is open! But running a store means lots of little jobs: answering the same questions, sending thank-you emails, counting orders. Real companies hand those jobs to AI helpers. In this module, you'll build your own.
```

```group
Pairs compare lists. Collect the most-circled jobs on the board.
```

```solo
You both make a list and swap. Pick one job from the student's list to map together later as an example.
```

## 2. Mini-lesson — Chatbot, automation or agent? (15 min · 1-on-1: 10 min)
Show **Visual 1**:

| | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 💬 **Chatbot** | Answers when you ask. Then waits. | You ask Claude to write a thank-you email. |
| ⚙️ **Automation** | Does fixed steps by itself when a trigger happens. Never thinks. | Every new order → the same thank-you email goes out. |
| 🤖 **AI agent** | Gets a goal, decides the steps, uses tools, checks its work. | "Answer new customer emails": it reads each one, picks the right info, writes a reply, and asks you before sending. |

**The workflow map** (**Visual 2**):
1. **Trigger:** what starts it?
2. **Steps:** what happens, in order?
3. **Tools:** what does each step use (email, sheet, form, Claude)?
4. **Check:** where does a human look before it matters?

**Good jobs for helpers:** they happen a lot, follow a pattern and are easy to check.
**Keep for humans:** big money choices, anything about a person's private info, anything where a mistake really hurts.

## 3. Hands-on build — Map your store's busy work (35 min · 1-on-1: 25 min)
1. List 6 or more busy jobs your store has.
2. For each one, guess: how often? how many minutes each time?
3. Pick the top 3 to hand to a helper.
4. Draw a workflow map for each of the 3 (trigger, steps, tools, check).
5. Ask Claude: "Here is my workflow map: [paste]. What step am I missing? Where could it go wrong?" Fix your map.

**Checkpoint 1 (+20 XP):** 6 jobs listed with how often and how long.
**Checkpoint 2 (+20 XP):** 3 workflow maps, each with a human check.

```group
Pairs swap maps and look for missing steps.
```

```solo
Play "the robot": the student reads their map step by step and you do EXACTLY what it says, nothing more. Wherever you get stuck or do something silly, a step is missing.
```

> 🛡️ **Privacy moment:** customer names, emails and addresses are private. Mark every step on your map that touches private info with a 🔒. Those steps need extra care.

## 4. Game / challenge — Robot or Human? (15 min · 1-on-1: 10 min)
Read each job. Should a **robot do it**, should a **robot help and a human check**, or should a **human do it**?
1. Send a thank-you email after every order. → Robot
2. Reply to a customer who says their bottle leaked and they're upset. → Robot drafts, human checks
3. Decide to give someone a full refund. → Human
4. Count this week's orders. → Robot
5. Post on social media for the brand. → Robot drafts, human checks
6. Pick which new product to make next. → Human (with AI ideas)
7. Sort emails into "question", "order problem" and "spam". → Robot (spot-check)
8. Answer "Where is my order?" with a tracking link. → Robot

```group
Houses vote. Best reasons: +15 XP. Everyone: +5.
```

```solo
**Hot seat.** The student answers each card in 10 seconds with a reason. Then they make up 2 new cards for you. +15 XP for playing, +5 for good reasons.
```

## 5. Share-out — Map tour (10 min · 1-on-1: 5 min)
Walk through your best workflow map. Where is the human check? Why there?

```solo
The student explains their best map. Ask: "What's the worst thing that could happen if the robot got this step wrong?"
```

## 6. Exit ticket (5 min)
1. What's the difference between an automation and an AI agent?
2. Name the 4 parts of a workflow map.
3. Name one job you'd never give to a robot. Why?

```solo
Ask out loud. Keep the 3 maps. You'll build them in the next lessons.
```

## Differentiation
- **Extra help:** map 1 workflow instead of 3. Use the example map as a pattern.
- **Extra challenge:** for each job, work out time saved per month (how often × minutes). Which helper saves the most?
- **1-on-1 tip:** if the student has a real job or club, map one of its busy jobs too. Real problems make people care the most.
